Granola alternatives: 4 honest picks for 2026

Granola alternatives compared for 2026: 4 honest options for AI meeting notes, from on-device Everpaper to Otter, Fireflies, and ScreenPipe.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 7 Oct 2026

Bias declared up front: Everpaper is our product. We build it, and we'd obviously like you to pick it. So we'll do the honest thing instead: name what Granola does better than everyone on this list, say plainly where Everpaper falls short, and let you match tools to your actual constraint.

Granola is a well-reviewed bot-free meeting notepad in 2026. Nat Friedman said it "generates the best meeting notes of anything I've tried," and the company raised a $125M Series C at a $1.5B valuation in March 2026. People don't leave Granola because it's bad. They leave for four specific reasons, and each points at a different alternative.

Why people leave Granola#

The audio leaves your machine. Granola's no-bot design captures mic and system audio locally, which is genuinely good. Then the audio streams to Deepgram or AssemblyAI for transcription, and the text goes to OpenAI or Anthropic for the notes. Transcripts and notes live in Granola's US-hosted AWS, encrypted at rest and in transit. It's SOC 2 Type 2 certified and careful. It's still cloud.

It trains on your anonymized data by default. OpenAI and Anthropic are barred from training on what Granola sends them, but Granola itself trains its own models on anonymized user data on the free and Business tiers. You can opt out in Settings; training is off by default only on Enterprise. (All from Granola's security page, checked 2026-08-04.)

There's nothing to play back. Granola stores no audio, ever. It transcribes in real time and keeps only the transcript and your notes. tldv's review called this "the no-recording catch": when a client disputes what was said, "the AI wrote it down" with no recording is a weak position.

Speaker labels and exports are thin. On desktop, the transcript labels everyone as "Me" (green bubbles) and "Them" (grey bubbles). Named speaker tags exist only for Google Meet, via a browser extension, and Zoom on macOS; Teams gets no named tags. A meetingnotes.com teardown scored Granola 66/100, citing "no export options and limited integrations" on the free tier.

If none of those bother you, stop reading and stay put. If one does, here are the exits.

The alternatives#

1. Everpaper — best for keeping the whole pipeline on your Mac#

Everpaper's meeting pillar, Meetings, is one of two options here where nothing leaves the machine by default. Transcription runs on-device: Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) writes the words and Nemotron labels speakers by default, Whisper is an option, and Detailed meetings re-listen with MOSS. No account, no sign-in, no stream to a transcription vendor. AI features like meeting Ask and summaries wait until you configure a provider, and the provider can be fully local: Everpaper Native (macOS 27), oMLX, LM Studio or Ollama. Cloud providers only work after you flip Allow cloud AI to analyze your journal, which is off by default. There's no training pipeline to opt out of because there's no account to train on.

It also fixes Granola's two functional gaps. Audio is kept locally as separate time-aligned mic and system tracks, so you can verify any quote against the recording. And speakers get live labels in the transcript on any platform, with a per-meeting option (Speakers tab, ⌘4) to hide your voice or any speaker from the transcript view; the audio and exports still keep everyone. With an AI provider set up, a finished meeting's action items show up in Today's focus; you can also run More → Extract To-Dos into Today on any meeting.

The honest limits: macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon only, no Windows, no mobile, no web. Private beta with limited spots, and no public price yet. AI features wait for a provider. And Granola's written notes are still better prose; Everpaper's automatic Summary is a working brief, not a colleague's write-up. MOSS wants 16 GB machines to be happy. Full detail in Granola vs Everpaper and how Meetings works.

2. Otter — best for feature-rich bot transcription across every platform#

Otter is the incumbent: a visible bot (OtterPilot) joins your Zoom, Meet, or Teams call, records, transcribes live, captures slides, and emails a summary. It runs on web, Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, and a Chrome extension, and Pro+ adds Salesforce, HubSpot, and Zapier integrations. Cross-meeting AI Chat over your whole archive is genuinely useful.

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04): Basic free with 300 monthly minutes, a 30-minute cap per conversation, and 3 lifetime file imports. Pro is $16.99/user/month, or $8.49/user/month billed annual. Business is $30/user/month, or $24 annual.

The disclosures you need before choosing it: the consolidated federal class action In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation (N.D. Cal.) alleges recording without all-party consent and using recordings to train AI; a motion-to-dismiss hearing was held May 20, 2026. Otter's privacy policy (June 16, 2026) reserves the right to train "proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions," and discloses data-labeling vendors. A 2022 HN report described the bot joining confidential meetings uninvited. If your meetings include people who haven't consented to a recording vendor, read those first. We cover this intent in depth in best Otter alternatives.

3. Fireflies — best for team analytics and CRM workflows#

Fireflies is the team play: its bot (Fred) joins calls, and the value is what happens after: conversation intelligence, team analytics, a task manager, and CRM push into HubSpot and Salesforce on every paid tier. Transcription covers 100+ languages, and the free tier is generous: unlimited transcription, 400 minutes of storage per team.

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04): Free $0. Pro $10/seat/month billed annually, $18 monthly. Business $19 annual / $29 monthly adds conversation intelligence and unlimited storage. Enterprise $39/seat/month, annual only, adds HIPAA BAA and SSO/SCIM.

Two disclosures. First, Cruz v. Fireflies.AI Corp., a BIPA class action filed December 2025 in the Central District of Illinois, alleges Fireflies collected voiceprints of meeting participants without the required notice and written consent; it's ongoing as of today. Second, the bot auto-joins via calendar sync by default, and reviews repeatedly complain it's hard to keep out of sensitive calls. To Fireflies' credit, its security page states "We don't train on it by default unlike other AI companies," with a 0-day retention policy with vendors. If you want the team features without the bot problem, none of these tools solve that; the bot is the product.

4. ScreenPipe — best for developers who want a local, scriptable recorder#

ScreenPipe is the open-source-flavored answer: 24/7 local capture of screen and mic, written in Rust, with data in ~/.screenpipe/db.sqlite, a local API at localhost:3030, and an MCP server for Claude, Cursor, and ChatGPT. Transcription is local Whisper by default; Deepgram is opt-in. PII redaction is a default-on flag. It runs on macOS, Windows, and Linux, and because it records system audio rather than joining calls, it works with any meeting platform, same trick as Granola.

Pricing checked 2026-08-04 against the supplied ScreenPipe pricing page: Free is $0 with local capture, AI search, meeting notes, 50+ integrations, and 10 AI credits/month. Basic is $25/month or $250/year with 150 credits and unlimited user-installed scheduled tasks and history. Business is $50/seat/month or $500/seat/year with 400 credits, frontier models, cloud sync, automations, and team workspace. The separate source license moved from MIT to a source-available commercial license for business use in June 2026.

The tradeoffs are real. It's a toolkit, not a notepad: you get raw capture and an agent framework ("pipes") rather than polished meeting notes, so expect to build your own summarization flow. Its own troubleshooting docs tell users to lower capture FPS and use whisper-tiny on older machines, which tells you about resource cost. And its community marketing got a ban request thread on r/LocalLLaMA in 2024. For a developer who wants a local memory layer, nothing else on this list comes close.

How to choose#

Start with the reason you're leaving, because each maps to one answer:

  • "Audio in someone else's cloud is the problem" → Everpaper if you're on an Apple Silicon Mac, ScreenPipe if you need Windows or Linux or want to script everything. These are the only two architectures here where the cloud path is off by default.
  • "I want more features, not more privacy" → Otter for the mature bot, cross-meeting chat, and Salesforce/HubSpot integrations; Fireflies if team analytics and conversation intelligence matter more than per-person polish.
  • "I need a recording to verify quotes" → anyone except Granola. Everpaper and ScreenPipe keep it local; Otter and Fireflies keep it in their clouds.

Two cross-cutting checks before you commit. First, consent: if your calls include clients, counsel, or anyone in Illinois or California, a bot that records by default (Otter, Fireflies) carries live litigation risk you should read about before rollout, and the BIPA and ECPA suits linked above are the starting points. Second, total cost: a 10-person team on Granola Business pays $1,680 over 12 months; the same team on Fireflies Pro annual pays $1,200; Otter Pro annual runs $1,018.80. Everpaper has no public price during the private beta.

FAQ#

Is there a bot-free Granola alternative?#

Two. Everpaper captures mic and system audio locally and transcribes on-device, with no bot and no cloud stream. ScreenPipe records whatever your machine hears and sees, also bot-free, and works on Mac, Windows, and Linux. Both keep the recording; Granola never does.

What's the cheapest Granola alternative?#

ScreenPipe's CLI is free with local transcription, and Fireflies' free tier gives unlimited transcription with 400 minutes of team storage (checked 2026-08-04). Otter's free tier is 300 minutes a month with a 30-minute cap per conversation. Everpaper is in private beta; we don't publish a price during beta.

Which Granola alternative is most private?#

Everpaper, structurally. Capture, OCR, transcription, storage, and search all run on-device; "no provider, no traffic" is the default, and an offline redaction pass scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs before any outbound AI call. ScreenPipe is second: local-first with default-on PII redaction and auditable source, though cloud AI is one flag away. Every other option on this list sends your audio to a vendor's cloud by design. The full model is in our privacy docs.

Can any alternative match Granola's note quality?#

Honestly, not today. Granola's human-in-the-loop notes, where you jot rough bullets and AI fleshes them out, remain the best-reviewed output in the category. Otter and Fireflies produce serviceable auto-summaries. Everpaper's meeting flow gives you a live working surface (speaker-labeled transcript, your notes, an "As we go" digest, Ask). With an AI provider it also writes an automatic Summary after the call, and you can export to txt, markdown, srt, vtt and json from the app or the CLI. The summary is a working brief, not Granola-style prose.

Do these work on Teams?#

All of them capture Teams audio one way or another. Granola, Everpaper, and ScreenPipe do it through system audio with no integration needed. Otter and Fireflies send a bot into the Teams call. The difference is speaker labels: Granola falls back to "Me"/"Them" on Teams, while Everpaper labels speakers live on any platform.

Can I move my Granola history to another tool?#

There's no bulk export path worth the name; the limited export options are part of why people leave. What transfers cleanly is future meetings. If you have transcript files from other tools in .vtt, Everpaper's Import Transcript (1.11.0) ingests them as real meetings with no audio needed.

Recommendation#

Stay with Granola if you want the best-written meeting notes in the category, you need Windows or mobile capture, and the cloud path doesn't bother you. Its security posture is genuinely good: SOC 2 Type 2, public post-mortems, no stored audio, providers barred from training. For a consultant living in Zoom on a Windows laptop, it's the right tool and the $14 Business tier is fairly priced.

Leave when the constraint is the data path itself. If meeting audio streaming to Deepgram and text flowing to OpenAI is a hard no, Everpaper is one of two options here where nothing leaves the Mac by default, and it adds the two things Granola can't: audio you can play back and speaker labels on every platform. If you need Windows or Linux with the same local-first posture and you're comfortable in a terminal, ScreenPipe. If you want team analytics and CRM plumbing and you accept the bot, Fireflies; if you want the mature incumbent feature set, Otter, eyes open on the litigation.

Everpaper's limits, again: macOS 15+, Apple Silicon only, no mobile or web, private beta, and the AI features wait for a provider you configure. If those constraints fit, apply for the private beta: limited spots, and if you're selected, you'll hear back within 48 hours.
