Fireflies.ai vs Everpaper: an honest 2026 comparison

Fireflies vs Everpaper compared honestly: a cloud meeting bot with CRM depth versus a private, on-device Mac daybook. Pricing, the BIPA suit, and who each fits.

By Marco Iannello · Updated August 4, 2026

Fireflies.ai is a cloud meeting bot that joins your calls, transcribes them in 100+ languages, and pushes the results into your CRM. Everpaper is a private, on-device daybook for macOS that records meetings from your own machine, with no bot in the participant list. Choose Fireflies for team workflows; choose Everpaper if the recording should never leave your Mac.

One disclosure before anything else: Everpaper is our product. We'll still tell you where Fireflies genuinely wins, because it does win in a few places.

Quick verdict

Choose Fireflies.ai if:

  • You need meeting notes pushed into Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, or Notion automatically.
  • Your team works across Windows, web, iOS, and Android, not just Mac.
  • You want a usable free tier and can live with a bot appearing in your calls.
  • You need compliance paperwork (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA BAA on Enterprise) for a company-wide rollout.

Choose Everpaper if:

  • You don't want a bot joining your meetings or your audio leaving your Mac.
  • You're on an Apple Silicon Mac (macOS 14.2+) and want transcription, search, and a day journal that work offline.
  • You prefer a founding-license option for early supporters to per-seat monthly billing.
  • You want speaker labels stored on your own Mac rather than in a vendor's cloud.

Head-to-head

Capture: a bot in the room vs no bot at all

Verdict: Everpaper, unless your workflow needs the bot.

Fireflies' default capture mechanism is a participant named Fred. Connect your calendar and it auto-joins your Zoom, Meet, and Teams calls — that behavior is on by default, and it's the single most common complaint in reviews: the bot shows up in sensitive calls uninvited, and users report it's hard to fully disable. Everyone else on the call sees it. Some of them won't be happy about it.

Everpaper has no bot because it isn't a cloud service. The Minutes records your microphone and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks, directly on your Mac. You start it with ⇧⌘M, the menu-bar item, or the Index (⌘K) → Begin minutes. If you want it tied to your calendar, Calendar Auto-Start is opt-in with read-only local access and a 5-second cancellable countdown toast before anything records. Events never leave the machine. There's no participant to explain, apologize for, or kick.

The honest counterpoint: Fireflies' bot means it can record calls you aren't even on your Mac for, from any device, and its Chrome extension and mobile apps cover in-person capture too. Everpaper records what's in front of your Mac. That's the whole trade.

Privacy and the BIPA lawsuit

Verdict: Everpaper, and this one isn't close.

In December 2025, Cruz v. Fireflies.AI Corp. was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of Illinois. The complaint alleges Fireflies collected and stored voiceprints — via its speaker-recognition diarization — of meeting participants without the notice and written consent Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act requires. Many of those participants never signed up for Fireflies at all; a bot joined their call. The case is ongoing as of 2026-08-04, with no settlement confirmed.

Fireflies' security page does say the right things: "You own your data. We don't train on it by default," 256-bit AES at rest, TLS in transit, SOC 2 Type II, and a 0-day retention policy with vendors. Enterprise adds HIPAA BAAs and private storage. That's a real compliance posture, better than most consumer notetakers.

Everpaper's answer is structural rather than contractual. Capture, OCR, transcription, storage, and search all happen on your Mac — the speech model runs on the Neural Engine, so there's no audio upload to encrypt in the first place. Speaker labels exist, but they live in your local database, never on a server where they could become a class action. An offline redaction pass at capture time scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs into [REDACTED:api_key]-style placeholders, and it runs again before any outbound AI call you opt into. "No provider, no traffic" is the default state.

Transcription and AI

Verdict: Fireflies for language breadth and team analytics; Everpaper for offline, speaker-labeled local capture.

Fireflies transcribes in 100+ languages in its cloud, with real-time notes, AskFred chat over your meetings, and conversation intelligence on Business and up. Reviews consistently flag accuracy drops with heavy accents, crosstalk, and specialized vocabulary — normal for the category, but plan on corrections.

Everpaper transcribes on-device with Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) as the default, Whisper as an option, and MOSS (transcription and diarization in one model, ~2 GB download, happiest on 16 GB machines) if you opt in. Live meetings get a floating panel with level meters, a live speaker-labeled transcript (⌘2), /decision /action /question snippets, and an Ask tab over the live transcript. Afterward, Extract todos pulls action items into Today's Focus automatically. The catch is real: Ask, summaries, and drafting wait until you configure a provider — a local one via Ollama, LM Studio, oMLX (with a one-click Qwen3.5 4B setup offered), or Apple Foundation Models, or a cloud one behind an explicit opt-in toggle that's off by default. Recording and search never need any of that.

Integrations and team features

Verdict: Fireflies, decisively. This is its home turf.

Fireflies is a workflow hub: HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack, Notion, Asana, unlimited integrations on Pro and up, and API access on every tier including free, with a documented GraphQL API. Business adds team analytics, user groups, and multi-language mode. If your goal is "every sales call lands in the CRM with action items," Fireflies is built for exactly that, and a solo on-device app can't match it.

Everpaper has no CRM integrations and no team features. What it has instead: export to a full Obsidian vault as linked Markdown, graph export as GraphML and Mermaid, meeting exports in txt/markdown/srt/vtt/json, and everpaper-cli in the app bundle for scripting your own pipelines (--json on everything, plus an llm group that takes a provider and key per call and stores nothing). If your destination is a personal knowledge base or your own tooling, that works. If your destination is Salesforce, it doesn't.

Beyond meetings

Verdict: different products wearing similar jackets.

Fireflies is a meeting tool. Everpaper is a daybook: alongside The Minutes, the Day Journal takes periodic screenshots of your front window (default every 2 minutes), OCRs them on-device, and writes an end-of-day narrative you can chat with. The Second Brain builds a local knowledge graph and a self-model — People, Files, Sites, Projects, Topics, each tied to exact moments, plus one-line Beliefs with (tentative)/(likely) confidence markers — gated behind a readiness threshold of 5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, and 3 recurring threads so it doesn't make things up on day one. ⇧⌘A asks your captured history and answers with sources you can click back to the exact frame; ⌥Space summons the Concordance search panel over any app. Screenshots are kept 30 days by default (7/30/90/Forever). None of that exists in Fireflies.

Use cases

Fireflies fits better when you're running a sales or CS team and meeting outcomes need to land in a CRM, when your org is mixed Windows/Mac/mobile, when you need 100+ language transcription, or when procurement requires SOC 2 reports and a BAA.

Everpaper fits better when you're one person (or a privacy-first team) on Apple Silicon Macs, your calls include people who never consented to a cloud recorder, you want the transcript plus searchable memory of your whole workday, or per-seat SaaS pricing for note-taking feels backwards to you.

Honest pros and cons

Fireflies.ai — pros

  • Genuinely generous free tier: unlimited transcription (rate-limited) and 400 mins of team storage for $0.
  • Broad integration and API coverage across paid tiers.
  • Broad platform coverage — web, desktop, iOS, Android, Chrome extension.
  • Real enterprise compliance: SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA BAA, SSO/SCIM, private storage.

Fireflies.ai — cons

  • The bot auto-joins by default and users report it's hard to fully disable.
  • Named defendant in an ongoing BIPA voiceprint class action filed December 2025.
  • Storage caps bite: 400 mins per team on free, 8,000 mins per seat on Pro.
  • Billing and AI-credit confusion is a recurring complaint, with higher-than-expected bills.

Everpaper — pros

  • No bot, no account, no upload — capture, transcription, search, and export all run locally.
  • Live speaker-labeled transcript with Hide my voice / Hide any speaker (⌘4) that persists.
  • More than meetings: Day Journal, Second Brain, verified answers with clickable frame sources.
  • Founding-license model for early supporters — not a monthly subscription.

Everpaper — cons

  • macOS 14.2+ on Apple Silicon only; no Intel, Windows, mobile, or web version.
  • Private beta with limited spots.
  • AI features (Ask, summaries, Reflection) wait for a provider you configure yourself.
  • No CRM or team integrations, and the Second Brain readiness gate takes about a week of real use.

FAQ

Is Fireflies.ai safe? Its security posture is real — SOC 2 Type II, encryption at rest and in transit, a stated no-training-on-your-data default, HIPAA BAAs on Enterprise. Recordings live in Fireflies' cloud, and the company is defending an ongoing BIPA class action (Cruz v. Fireflies.AI, filed December 2025) alleging it collected meeting participants' voiceprints without consent. Those are separate architectural and legal considerations.

Does Fireflies join meetings automatically? Yes. With calendar integration connected, the Fred bot auto-joins your meetings by default, and it appears in the participant list like any attendee. You can tune auto-join rules in settings, but multiple reviews note that fully disabling it is harder than it should be — which is how bots end up in calls nobody invited them to.

Does Everpaper work on Windows or mobile? No. It requires macOS 14.2 or later on Apple Silicon. There's no iOS, Windows, Linux, Android, or web version.

Can Everpaper push meeting notes to Salesforce or HubSpot? The supplied Everpaper docs do not list Salesforce or HubSpot connectors. They do document Markdown/GraphML exports, an Obsidian vault export, and everpaper-cli with --json output for user-built automation.

What happens to my Everpaper data if I stop using it? Nothing to cancel — there's no account and nothing on a server. Your journal, meetings, and graph are files on your Mac, and plain-Markdown export means no lock-in.

Is Everpaper free? Everpaper has no public price. It is in private beta with a founding-license option for early supporters, not a monthly subscription. Join the waitlist for beta updates.

Final recommendation

If you need meeting intelligence piped into a CRM across a mixed-device team, Fireflies.ai is the right tool and its free tier is a fair way to test it — just go in knowing a bot joins by default, storage is metered, and a biometric-privacy lawsuit is pending over how it handles voices.

If the reason you're reading this is that a bot showed up in a call it shouldn't have, or the BIPA filing made you re-read your notetaker's security page, Everpaper uses a different architecture: your meetings and workday are captured, transcribed, and searched on your Mac, with the whole meetings flow documented and no vendor-side recording archive.
