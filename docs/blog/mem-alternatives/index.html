Mem alternatives: 4 honest options in 2026

Looking for Mem alternatives? Four honest picks for AI notes in 2026 — on-device, encrypted, and local-first options, with real pricing and tradeoffs.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 8 Oct 2026

Mem pitched itself as "the world's first self-organizing workspace" when Mem X launched in November 2022, backed by a $23.5M Series A led by the OpenAI Startup Fund. Four years later, the pitch is the same but a lot of the early believers have moved on. If you're one of them, this page is for you.

Full disclosure up front: Everpaper is our product, and it's the first alternative below. We'll be plain about where it wins, where it loses, and when you should stay with Mem instead.

Why people leave Mem#

The complaints cluster around a few recurring themes, most of them documented in Mem's own FAQ and in r/PKMS threads.

It's cloud-only, and it's not end-to-end encrypted — by design. Mem's official pricing FAQ says it word for word: "Your data is encrypted at rest with AES-256 and in transit with TLS, but it is not e2e encrypted. This is because our core focus is to provide you with intelligence on top of your Mem." Read that again. Mem's servers can read your notes. That's not an oversight or a roadmap gap — it's the architecture. For a tool people use as a second brain, that's a hard line for a lot of privacy-conscious users.

Google-only sign-in. From the same FAQ: only people with Google or Google Workspace accounts can sign into Mem. No Apple sign-in, no email-and-password, no passkeys. If you don't want your knowledge base tied to a Google identity, you're done before you start.

The $99/month Proactive tier. Mem Pro is a reasonable $12/month (checked 2026-08-04). But the features Mem markets hardest — the agent that tracks open loops, briefs you before meetings, and resurfaces what matters — live behind Mem Proactive at $99/month after a 7-day trial. That's $1,188 a year for a notes app.

Stalled-development sentiment. r/PKMS threads through 2025 report bugs, slow updates, a historically missing Android app, and an editor that still trails Notion and Obsidian on basics. Users talk about "Mem 2.0" as a rework that took a long time to arrive. Meanwhile the core promise — that Mem organizes your notes for you — keeps getting flagged as underdelivered: the self-organization is still incomplete and buggy in practice, per long-running community criticism. There's also an aggregated trail of billing and support complaints on review sites.

Nothing works offline. No local vault, no offline mode. Plane, cabin, dead zone — your second brain is unreachable.

If those tradeoffs no longer work for you, here are the exits.

1. Everpaper — best for automatic capture with on-device answers#

Everpaper takes the opposite bet from Mem on almost every axis. Where Mem asks you to capture notes into its cloud and then read them back through its servers, Everpaper captures your work automatically and keeps everything on your Mac.

The capture piece is the big difference. Mem's model still assumes you'll stop what you're doing and write something down. Everpaper's Day Journal takes a full-screen screenshot every 2 minutes by default (or, in Activity only mode, keeps an app-and-window timeline with no screenshots), runs OCR on-device, and writes an AI narrative of your day you can ask about at the end of it. Its meeting recorder captures your Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby calls — mic and system audio as separate tracks — and transcribes on-device: Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) writes the words and Nemotron labels speakers, with no audio leaving the machine. Memory (also called Second Brain) builds a knowledge graph of people, files, sites, and projects from all of it, and Ask your memory… (⇧⌘A) answers questions in streamed prose with clickable sources, each linking back to the exact frame it came from. Memory's Ask tab also checks its answers against their sources and removes unsupported sentences (the check isn't perfect, so the sources are shown).

On privacy, there's no carve-out. Capture, OCR, transcription, storage, search, and the knowledge graph all run locally with no account and no sign-in. AI features that need an LLM wait until you configure a provider — Everpaper Native (macOS 27), oMLX, LM Studio or Ollama keep everything on-device; cloud providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter) only work after you flip Allow cloud AI to analyze your journal, which is off by default. An offline redaction pass scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs at capture time and again before anything goes to a cloud model.

Drawbacks, honestly: macOS 15+ only, Apple Silicon only — no Windows, no mobile, no web app, so it's useless as a capture-everywhere tool. It's in private beta with limited spots — not something you can just buy today. Memory needs about 5 days of capture (300 frames, 60 distinct things, 3 recurring threads) before it reads Ready. And it's not a manual note-taking app — if writing and linking notes by hand is the point for you, Obsidian below is the better tool.

Best for: Mac users who liked Mem's "AI over my knowledge" idea but want the capture to be automatic and the data to never leave their machine.

2. Obsidian — best for total ownership if you'll do the work#

Obsidian is the answer the r/PKMS privacy crowd lands on most often, and for good reason: your notes are plain-text Markdown files in a folder on your disk, readable by any tool, forever. The app is free for personal and commercial use — since February 2025 there's no required license at all, no account, no telemetry, no ads. Obsidian's own pricing page states your data is stored locally and inaccessible to them.

The AI story is bring-your-own. The core app ships zero AI features. Community plugins fill the gap: Smart Connections does embeddings-based related notes and chat with a local embedding model by default — no API key, nothing sent out — while Copilot-style plugins send your note context to whatever LLM provider you configure, including local models through Ollama. Whether your data leaves the device is entirely your call, plugin by plugin. That's the opposite of Mem, where cloud processing is mandatory and non-negotiable.

Sync is the main paid piece: Obsidian Sync Standard is $4/user/mo billed annually (1 vault, 1 GB, E2E encrypted) and Plus is $8/user/mo (10 vaults, 10 GB) — checked 2026-08-04. Or you skip it and sync through iCloud, Syncthing, or git for free.

Drawbacks: everything is manual. No automatic capture, no meeting recording, no transcription anywhere in the product. You write every note yourself, you build the structure yourself, and the learning curve (Markdown, vaults, wikilinks, plugin configuration) is real — "what's bad about Obsidian" threads are a genre on r/ObsidianMD. The mobile app is a frequent complaint: slow startup and lag on large vaults. If Mem's appeal was that it organized things for you, Obsidian will feel like going back to doing it all by hand.

Best for: people who want a second brain they fully own, on every platform, and who enjoy building their own system.

3. Reflect — best for encrypted notes that still have built-in AI#

Reflect is the closest thing to "Mem, but encrypted." It's a networked notes app built around a daily note, with backlinks, a fast minimalist editor, and an AI palette (⌘J) for transforming text and chatting with your notes. Crucially, your notes are end-to-end encrypted client-side with XChaCha20-Poly1305 before they hit Reflect's servers — their security page says the stored data is "an encrypted blob which we can't read," and there's a published Doyensec audit backing the cryptography. One plan, $10/month billed annually, 14-day trial, no free tier (checked 2026-08-04).

Now the honest carve-out, because it matters for exactly the audience leaving Mem: Reflect's AI features punch through the encryption boundary. Text you highlight for the AI palette goes to OpenAI or Anthropic; AI chat uses those providers plus Google Gemini; voice memo transcription sends audio to OpenAI's Whisper API. Reflect discloses all of this, and their privacy policy states plainly that "OpenAI and Anthropic may use prompts we send them as training data." So notes at rest are encrypted, but anything you run through the AI features leaves in plaintext. That's a defensible tradeoff — it's disclosed, it's scoped to what you explicitly select — but it's a tradeoff. AI usage is capped at 100k tokens/day on paid plans unless you add your own API key.

Drawbacks: Apple-only plus web — no Windows or Android app, a recurring dealbreaker. The client is closed-source, so you're trusting the binary (Reflect itself admits "ultimately you have to trust the client"). Lose your password and your notes are gone permanently — that's the price of real E2EE. And capture is manual: no meeting bot, no automatic anything beyond calendar event import and Kindle highlights.

Best for: Apple-ecosystem users who want Mem-style AI chat over their notes with real encryption at rest, and can live with the disclosed AI carve-out.

4. Day One — best if what you actually wanted was a journal#

Some people leaving Mem realize their use was never knowledge management — it was recording their days. If that's you, Day One is the mature option: 15 years of development, owned by Automattic since 2021, and E2E encrypted by default on journals created after v4.2, with the key escrowed to your own iCloud or Google Drive. Their privacy FAQ is blunt: "Will Day One ever sell my journal data? No, never."

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04): Basic is free with unlimited entries and 1 photo each; Silver is $49.99/year for unlimited media, multi-device sync, and audio recording with transcription; Gold is $74.99/year and added AI features in April 2026 — Daily Chat guided reflection, entry summaries, title suggestions, image generation. Platform coverage is the broadest on this list: iOS, macOS, Android, Windows, watchOS, and a web app.

Drawbacks: it's a journal, not a second brain. No backlinks-and-graph PKM, no meeting capture, no semantic chat across a knowledge base. Capture is manual — you write entries, attach photos, record audio. Sync runs on Day One's own cloud (they moved away from iCloud years ago, which some long-time users still resent), and r/dayoneapp has a standing genre of sync-reliability complaints plus a historical data-loss incident that burned trust for some. The official Day One pages reviewed for this article do not state where Day One's own Gold-tier AI features are processed.

Best for: people whose "notes" were really a diary, and who want prompts, streaks, photos, and printed books more than a knowledge graph.

How to choose#

Work backwards from why you're leaving.

  • Leaving over privacy and Google-only sign-in? Everpaper (nothing leaves the Mac, no account) or Obsidian (local files, free) fix it completely. Reflect fixes it at rest with a disclosed AI exception.
  • Leaving because the self-organization underdelivered? Decide whether you want automation done differently (Everpaper captures everything and builds the graph itself) or structure you control (Obsidian, where the organization is yours by construction).
  • Leaving over the $99 Proactive tier? Reflect's $120 annual plan and Day One Gold at $74.99 per year cost far less than Mem Proactive's $1,188 annual total. Obsidian's core app is free, with Sync optional. Everpaper has no public beta price.
  • Leaving because capture felt like homework? Everpaper is the only option on this list that captures your work and meetings automatically. Everything else still asks you to write.
  • Realizing you were journaling, not building a knowledge base? Day One, and don't look back.

FAQ#

Is Mem encrypted? Partially. Mem's own pricing FAQ says data is "encrypted at rest with AES-256 and in transit with TLS, but it is not e2e encrypted." Mem's servers can read your notes to power the AI features — that's a stated design choice, not a bug. If you want a provider that can't read your content, you need on-device processing (Everpaper, Obsidian) or real E2EE (Reflect, Day One).

Is Mem worth $12 a month in 2026? If you live in Google's ecosystem, capture notes constantly, and genuinely use Mem Agent's chat across your knowledge base, Pro at $12/month (checked 2026-08-04) is a fair price — reviewers consistently praise the "toss it in, find it later" flow. It's the $99/month Proactive tier and the privacy architecture that push people to look around.

Can I get my data out of Mem? Yes — Mem's official FAQ promises one-click export of all notes and collections to Markdown files. That export drops cleanly into Obsidian, which makes it the lowest-friction migration target.

What's the most private Mem alternative? Everpaper and Obsidian, in different ways. Everpaper processes everything on your Mac — capture, on-device transcription, search, knowledge graph — with no account at all, and cloud AI is off until you explicitly enable it. Obsidian keeps your notes as local files and never sees them; AI privacy depends on which plugins you configure. Reflect encrypts notes at rest but sends AI-selected text to OpenAI/Anthropic/Google, disclosed in its own privacy policy.

Is there a free Mem alternative? Obsidian is free for personal and commercial use with no account. Day One has a usable free tier (unlimited entries, one device). Mem's own free tier — 25 notes, 25 Mem Agent messages, 25 PDF pages a month (checked 2026-08-04) — is effectively a trial by comparison.

Does any Mem alternative record meetings? Everpaper does — it recognizes Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby links, records mic and system audio as separate tracks, and transcribes on-device. Mem itself only offers Meeting Briefings (context before a call); it never joins or records one. The other tools on this list don't record meetings either.

Our recommendation#

If you're leaving Mem over the privacy architecture — the not-E2EE FAQ admission, the Google-only sign-in, the cloud-only design — pick the tool that removes the question entirely. On a Mac, that's Everpaper: automatic capture of your screen and meetings, a second brain built from your actual work, and AI answers drawn from your own captured history, with sources, all on-device with no account. We built it, so weigh that — but also weigh that every privacy claim above traces to our docs, not to marketing.

If you'd rather own plain files and build the system yourself, Obsidian is free and will outlive every app on this list. If you want Mem's AI polish with real encryption at rest, Reflect is the honest middle ground.

Stay with Mem if fast, frictionless manual capture into searchable AI chat is genuinely all you need, the $12/month Pro tier covers your usage, and you've read the encryption FAQ and made peace with it. That's a legitimate choice. A lot of people make it.

Everyone else: Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots. Apply for the private beta; if you're selected, you'll hear back within 48 hours. For the head-to-head, read Mem vs Everpaper, and for how the on-device knowledge layer works, see the Memory docs.
