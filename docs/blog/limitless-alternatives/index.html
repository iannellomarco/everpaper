Limitless alternatives: 5 honest options in 2026

Limitless alternatives after Meta's acquisition: Everpaper, ScreenPipe, Granola, Otter, and Fireflies, with dated pricing, privacy tradeoffs, and clear picks.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 7 Oct 2026

Limitless users didn't choose to shop for an alternative. The choice was made for them. On December 5, 2025, Meta announced it was acquiring Limitless, the company founded as Rewind AI in 2020 and rebranded in 2024. Pendant sales stopped the same day. The desktop and web apps lost recording. Users in the EU, UK, Brazil, China, Israel, South Korea, and Turkey were cut off with two weeks' notice, data deleted after December 19, 2025.

What's left is a service in sunset: existing customers get the Unlimited plan free, with support committed "throughout 2026." That's not a roadmap. That's a bridge, and bridges are for crossing, not living on.

A quick disclosure before anything else: Everpaper is our product. It sits first because we built it for someone who wants a private record of their days without handing everything to a cloud provider. Read that entry with the appropriate grain of salt. Everything else comes from dated competitor research, with prices stamped where we could verify them (checked 2026-08-04).

Why people are leaving Limitless#

Usually a "why people leave X" section lists grievances. Here it's simpler: the product is ending.

The Meta acquisition. Announced 2025-12-05, covered by TechCrunch the same day. Meta's stated rationale is "personal superintelligence" and AI wearables; the Limitless team joins Meta. The official Limitless FAQ gives no product roadmap beyond its 2026 support commitment.

The Pendant is no longer sold. Sales halted December 5, 2025. The device launched at $99 in April 2024. Existing owners get Unlimited free and support "throughout 2026." There is no official commitment beyond that window.

The apps stopped recording. The Mac/Windows desktop apps and the web app at app.limitless.ai had recording disabled after the acquisition. You can access past recordings through 2026; you can't make new ones. For a product whose entire premise was capture, that's the end of the product in every sense that matters.

Seven country and regional markets were cut off. Users in the EU, UK, Brazil, China, Israel, South Korea, and Turkey lost access on December 5, 2025. Their data was deleted after December 19. Limitless provided an export tool, but two weeks is a short migration window for a memory archive.

The privacy story changed. Rewind built its name on on-device processing. Limitless moved to cloud processing with TLS in transit, HSM-backed storage, and configurable retention. Remaining users must accept updated terms tied to Meta.

The free Unlimited plan is a bridge. Existing customers no longer pay a subscription during the support window. Using that access while testing replacements is reasonable. Depending on it past 2026 isn't.

The alternatives#

1. Everpaper — best for Limitless users who want the on-device successor#

Ours, as declared. Everpaper follows the original Rewind idea that a Mac can remember your day while the record stays local.

Everpaper is a private, on-device daybook for macOS. Capture, OCR, transcription, storage, search, and its knowledge graph run locally without an account. AI features wait for a provider. Local options include Everpaper Native (macOS 27), oMLX, LM Studio and Ollama. Cloud AI stays off until the user enables it, and an offline redaction pass scrubs secrets before outbound calls.

For meetings, ⇧⌘M starts a recording. It records mic and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks without a bot and transcribes on-device: Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) writes the words and Nemotron labels speakers by default, Whisper is an option, and Detailed meetings re-listen with MOSS. Recall is available through the Index with ⌘K or Concordance with ⌥Space. Version 1.11.0's Import Transcript accepts .vtt files from Zoom, Teams, or Otter. The journal exports as linked Markdown.

The limits matter: macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only, with no Windows, mobile, web, or Pendant equivalent. Everpaper is in private beta. Its Memory (also called Second Brain) waits for 5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, and 3 recurring threads before it reports Ready. AI features need a provider.

There's no public price during the beta.

Best for: Apple Silicon Mac users whose Limitless use lived on the desktop, and who want the record to stay on their machine this time. Head-to-head: Limitless vs Everpaper.

2. ScreenPipe — best for tinkerers who want raw 24/7 capture, cross-platform#

ScreenPipe is the closest living relative of Limitless's desktop capture. It records screen and mic, performs OCR and local Whisper transcription, exposes a local API at localhost:3030, and supports scheduled AI agents called pipes. Capture, search, and transcription can work offline on macOS, Windows, and Linux, though Linux support varies by compositor.

It is closer to infrastructure than a finished meeting product. Users assemble summaries and filing workflows through pipes, and continuous capture costs CPU and battery. Its license changed from MIT to the source-available Screenpipe Commercial License on 2026-06-09; commercial use of the source now requires a paid license.

Pricing checked 2026-08-04: the CLI is free, as is a self-built desktop app for personal use. The signed desktop app is paid, but its current official price couldn't be verified from a machine-readable page. Third-party prices conflict, so we won't quote one.

Best for: developers who liked Limitless's platform-agnostic capture and want to build on raw local data, including on Windows or Linux. See our ScreenPipe vs Everpaper comparison for the build-vs-buy framing.

3. Granola — best if meetings were your whole Limitless use case#

Granola captures microphone and system audio without a meeting bot. You write rough notes during the call, then its AI expands them using the transcript. It supports macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android and raised a $125M Series C at a $1.5B valuation in March 2026.

It is cloud-based. Transcription uses Deepgram or AssemblyAI; summarization uses OpenAI or Anthropic; notes live in US-hosted AWS. Audio isn't stored, but consumer-tier training on anonymized data is on by default and can be disabled in Settings. Desktop speaker labels default to “Me” and “Them,” with named speakers on Google Meet and Zoom through extensions. Granola handles meetings, not a day journal or wearable capture.

Pricing checked 2026-08-04: Basic is free with limited meeting history; Granola's official pricing page doesn't publish the exact cap. Business is $14 per user monthly and Enterprise is $35 per user monthly. No annual discount was shown.

Best for: ex-Limitless users who only ever wanted meeting notes, don't mind cloud processing, and want an active meeting-notes product. If that's you, our Granola vs Everpaper page goes deeper.

4. Otter.ai — the incumbent, bot and baggage included#

Otter covers web, Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. OtterPilot joins Zoom, Meet, and Teams as a visible participant, transcribes live, captures slides, and sends a summary. Its integrations include Salesforce, HubSpot, and Zapier.

That bot model creates a consent burden. Otter also faces In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation, a consolidated federal class action alleging recording without all-party consent and training on recordings. A motion-to-dismiss hearing was held May 20, 2026; no outcome is implied here. Otter's privacy policy effective June 16, 2026 permits training on de-identified recordings and transcripts.

Pricing checked 2026-08-04: Basic is free with 300 minutes monthly and a 30-minute conversation cap. Pro is $16.99 per user monthly or $8.49 billed annually. Business is $30 monthly or $24 billed annually. Enterprise is custom.

Best for: teams that need broad platform and CRM coverage and accept a visible bot plus the pending consent case. The full intent lives on our best Otter alternatives 2026 page.

5. Fireflies.ai — same shape as Otter, different lawsuit#

Fireflies' bot, Fred, joins Zoom, Meet, Teams, and Webex. It offers summaries, action items, AskFred, and transcription in more than 100 languages. Its security page says customers own their data and training is off by default.

Calendar auto-join can create the same uninvited-bot problem as Otter. Fireflies also faces Cruz v. Fireflies.AI Corp., filed December 2025, which alleges voiceprint-based speaker identification violated Illinois BIPA consent requirements. The case remained ongoing at the research date; no settlement was confirmed.

Pricing checked 2026-08-04: Free includes 400 minutes of team storage. Pro is $10 per seat monthly on annual billing or $18 monthly. Business is $19 annual or $29 monthly. Enterprise is $39 per seat monthly, billed annually.

Best for: teams that want a broad meeting-bot feature set and accept a pending biometric-privacy case.

How to choose#

Start with what you actually used Limitless for, because "Limitless user" covered two different people.

If you lived in the desktop app: your options split on cloud versus local processing. Granola offers bot-free cloud meetings, while Otter and Fireflies use meeting bots. Everpaper is a Mac-only private beta; ScreenPipe is a cross-platform toolkit. Both keep the capture record on your machine. The Rewind alternatives page covers the same displaced user group from the earlier product's side.

If you wore the Pendant: nothing on this list replaces hands-free, in-person wearable capture. Everpaper and ScreenPipe cover computer activity. Granola, Otter, and Fireflies cover meeting workflows, but none matches the retired hardware's form factor. If wearable capture was essential, the honest answer is that this set has no direct replacement.

Use the bridge deliberately. The free Unlimited plan through 2026 is genuinely useful: export your archive now via the Help Center tool, keep using it while you trial replacements, and be migrated before the support window closes. Don't let "free until 2026" turn into "scrambling in January 2027."

FAQ#

Is Limitless shutting down? Yes, it is winding down on a defined support window. Meta's acquisition was announced December 5, 2025. Pendant sales stopped that day, desktop and web recording are disabled, and the EU, UK, Brazil, China, Israel, South Korea, and Turkey lost access with data deleted after December 19, 2025. Existing customers get Unlimited free and support "throughout 2026" per the official FAQ. No commitment exists past 2026.

What replaces the Limitless Pendant? Nothing in this set matches the Pendant's hands-free, in-person wearable capture. Everpaper and ScreenPipe cover activity at a computer. Granola, Otter, and Fireflies focus on meetings. If the wearable form factor was essential, use the 2026 support bridge while you assess products outside this list.

Can I export my data from Limitless? Yes. Limitless published an export tool through its Help Center (help.limitless.ai) as part of the wind-down. Do it now rather than waiting until December 2026; users in the cut-off markets received only two weeks' notice before deletion.

What's the most private Limitless alternative? Everpaper, if you're on an Apple Silicon Mac: capture, OCR, on-device transcription, storage, knowledge graph, and search all run locally with no account, cloud AI is off by default behind an explicit toggle, and a redaction pass scrubs secrets at capture time and again before any outbound AI call. ScreenPipe's local mode is comparably private if you stay off its cloud features. Every other option here is a cloud product.

Is there a free Limitless alternative? Several have real free tiers, checked 2026-08-04: Limitless itself (Unlimited free for existing users through 2026), ScreenPipe's CLI (free, local), Granola Basic (free, limited history), Otter Basic (300 minutes/mo), Fireflies Free (unlimited rate-limited transcription, 400 mins storage per team). Everpaper is in private beta with no public price yet.

What happened to Rewind? Same company, same story, earlier chapter. Rewind AI rebranded to Limitless in 2024, and the Rewind Mac app stopped recording on December 19, 2025. The rewind.ai domain is now an unaffiliated AI-tools aggregator. Our Rewind alternatives page is the migration guide for that side of the displaced user base.

Should I just wait to see what Meta ships? No replacement from Meta had been announced as of 2026-08-04. Waiting is reasonable only after you export your archive and put current capture somewhere else. The verified commitment covers Limitless support throughout 2026, not a successor or a 2027 service.

Recommendation#

Stay with Limitless if you're an existing customer riding out the free Unlimited window while you evaluate. Export your data today and use the 2026 support period as a migration bridge.

Move to Everpaper if you're on an Apple Silicon Mac and want to avoid another cloud-dependent memory archive. It offers on-device capture and transcription, no account, local AI options, and Markdown export during its private beta. The declared limits remain: Mac-only, Apple Silicon only, no wearable or mobile app, and a readiness gate on Memory.

Move to Granola if meetings were your whole use case and cloud processing is acceptable. Move to ScreenPipe if you're technical, want cross-platform raw capture, and enjoy owning the pipeline. Move to Otter or Fireflies if you want the established bot-based incumbents with eyes open about the consent litigation both are facing.

Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots. Apply for the private beta; if you're selected, you'll hear back within 48 hours. Or read the meetings documentation and the privacy model to see what the on-device successor looks like.
