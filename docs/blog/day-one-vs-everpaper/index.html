Day One vs Everpaper: an honest comparison for 2026

Day One vs Everpaper, compared honestly in 2026: manual AI-assisted journaling vs an automatic on-device daybook for macOS. Pricing, privacy, use cases.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 8 Oct 2026

Everpaper is our product. We'll say that once, up front, and then be as fair as we can, because Day One is a mature journal app. Comparing it to Everpaper is really comparing two different answers to the question "how do I remember my life?"

Day One's answer: you sit down and write. It launched in 2011, claims 4M+ journals created, and added a Gold tier in April 2026 ($74.99/yr) with AI summaries and Daily Chat. Everpaper's answer: your Mac records what you did through screenshots of your screen, on-device OCR, and meeting transcription, then writes the day locally with no account.

Different philosophies. For some people they're complementary. For others, one clearly replaces the other.

Quick verdict#

Choose Day One if…

  • You journal to process feelings, and the act of writing is the point
  • You want one journal across iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows, Mac, and web
  • Media-rich entries (up to 30 photos/videos, audio, drawings, scans) matter to you
  • You want printed hardcover books of your journal at a subscriber discount

Choose Everpaper if…

  • You keep meaning to journal but never sit down to do it
  • You want the record of what you did (apps, files, meetings, sites) not what you felt
  • You want meeting recording and on-device transcription built into the same app
  • You want everything local: no account, no cloud sync, no subscription

How we compared#

We read Day One's current plans page, privacy FAQ, and known-issues page (all checked 2026-08-04), the 9to5Mac report on the April 2026 Gold launch, and long-running user threads on r/dayoneapp about sync and pricing. For Everpaper we used the shipped docs: every feature claim below traces to a real settings pane, shortcut, or release note. Where Day One's official pages don't disclose a processing detail, we state that absence.

Head-to-head#

What gets captured#

Verdict: no overlap, by design.

Day One captures what you bring to it. Typed entries, photos and videos (1 per entry free, 30 per entry paid), audio recordings with transcription on Silver and up, drawings, document scans, templates, and assistive capture like location tagging, Apple Journaling Suggestions, and email/SMS journaling. It's rich, as long as you show up.

Everpaper captures what happened whether you show up or not. The Day Journal takes a full-screen screenshot every 2 minutes by default (30 s to 10 min), OCRs it on-device, reviews new activity every 10 minutes (10/15/30/60), and writes the day up as a first-person entry when you stop. If you'd rather keep no screenshots, Activity only mode records an app-and-window timeline instead. A Mark Moment button pins anything with an optional note. Pause reasons cover Meeting, Break, Lunch, Personal, or custom; Pause when you’re away is on by default at 2 minutes.

The honest summary: Day One is a better record of your inner life. Everpaper is a better record of your outer one.

Cross-platform reach#

Verdict: Day One, decisively.

Day One runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Android, Windows, and a web app at dayone.me. Its sync is end-to-end encrypted with AES-GCM-256 (encrypted before leaving your device, key never sent to Day One's servers), though note the caveat: E2E is default-on only for journals created after v4.2 on iOS/Mac; Android and older journals need manual opt-in.

Everpaper is macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon only. No iOS app. No web app. No sync; there is no server. If your journaling habit lives on your phone during the commute, Day One is the only option here. If your workday happens at a Mac, Everpaper's single-platform limit may not matter to you.

Privacy model#

Verdict: different definitions of "private," both credible.

Day One's posture is genuinely strong for a cloud app. E2E encryption by default on newer journals, the key escrowed to your iCloud or Google Drive, a flat "No, never!" on selling journal data, and it works without an account entirely, "like having a physical notebook." The remaining trust surface is the sync service itself: Day One moved off iCloud to its own cloud, which some long-time users still resent, and sync-reliability complaints (lost edits, cross-device failures) recur on r/dayoneapp alongside a standing known-issues page.

Everpaper removes the sync question: there is no account and nothing to sync to. Capture, OCR, transcription (Parakeet parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3, on-device), storage, search, and recall cards all run locally. "No provider, no traffic": AI features wait until you configure one, and a redaction pass scrubs keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs before anything leaves. Local AI options (Everpaper Native, oMLX, LM Studio, Ollama) keep even that on the Mac; cloud providers sit behind an off-by-default toggle.

Day One encrypts your journal on the way to its cloud. Everpaper never sends its core journal pipeline anywhere. More on Everpaper's model in the privacy docs.

AI features#

Verdict: Day One's AI is more turnkey; Everpaper's is more optional and more local.

Day One's Gold tier ($74.99/yr, launched April 2026) added Daily Chat (guided reflection), reflective summaries and prompts, Go Deeper prompts, Entry Summaries, Title Suggestions, Image Generation, Apple Intelligence features, and Day One Labs. Daily usage limits apply. The official pages reviewed don't disclose processing for the non-Apple-Intelligence AI features.

Everpaper's AI is a layer you switch on. The day's written entry is the only capture-path feature that needs a provider; capture, OCR, search, and storage never do. Ask your memory… (⇧⌘A) streams an answer written only from matching days and frames, each with a clickable source: the exact frame, with time, app, and window title. The Concordance (⌥Space) floats search and ask over any app. In Memory's Ask tab, answers are checked against their sources, and unsupported sentences are removed (the check isn't perfect, so the sources are shown). But until you connect a provider, none of the prose features exist.

Meetings#

Verdict: Everpaper, unopposed.

Day One has no meeting features: no recording, no call awareness. It's not that kind of app.

Everpaper's meeting recorder recognizes Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby links. Start with ⇧⌘M, the Record tile, or the Index (⌘K) → Begin meeting. Microphone and system audio record as separate time-aligned tracks, and transcription runs on-device with speaker labels. With an AI provider, a finished meeting's action items show up in Today's focus, and More → Extract To-Dos into Today does it on demand. Details are in the meeting docs.

What you can do with the archive#

Verdict: Day One for keepsakes, Everpaper for recall.

Day One's signature output is physical: printed hardcover and paperback journal books, 25–35% off for subscribers, plus PDF and CSV/JSON export. On This Day and streaks reward the long habit.

Everpaper's output is computational: export the whole journal as a linked Obsidian vault (Days/, People/, Links/, Files/, Topics/, GRAPH_REPORT.md), export the knowledge graph as GraphML or Mermaid, or script it with everpaper-cli and its --json output. Memory (also called Second Brain) builds a local knowledge graph (People, Files, Sites, Topics, beliefs with tentative/likely confidence markers) after a readiness gate of 5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, and 3 recurring threads. See the Memory docs and export docs.

By default Everpaper keeps 30 days: on a new install, screenshots and the day's text expire together. To keep your days, choose Forever or turn off Also delete their text & recall data, or export them to Obsidian. Meetings aren't affected.

Use cases#

Day One wins outright:

  • The reflective journaler. You write to think. Daily Chat and Go Deeper prompts are built for exactly this.
  • The multi-device diarist. Entry starts on iPhone, photo added on iPad, reviewed on the web.
  • The memory-keeper. Trip journals full of photos, printed into a book every year.

Everpaper wins outright:

  • The person whose "journal" would otherwise be nothing. You won't write entries; your Mac writes the day anyway.
  • Anyone in back-to-back calls. Recordings, transcripts, action items, on-device.
  • The recall-driven worker. "What was that number on the pricing page Tuesday?" Ask the Book answers with the source frame.

Complementary, honestly: write feelings in Day One on your phone at night; let Everpaper hold the factual record of the workday on your Mac. Neither app does the other's job.

Honest pros and cons#

Day One#

Pros:

  • Fifteen years of development, with 200k+ 5-star ratings claimed
  • Real E2E encryption (AES-GCM-256) with the key held in your iCloud or Google Drive
  • Works fully offline without an account
  • Broad media support: 30 photos/videos per entry, audio + transcription, drawings, scans, printed books

Cons:

  • The good stuff costs $49.99–$74.99 every year; free tier is 1 photo per entry and 1 device
  • Gold's AI features carry daily usage limits
  • Recurring sync-failure and lost-edit reports, plus a standing known-issues page across platforms
  • Proprietary sync replaced iCloud, a direction long-time users still complain about

Everpaper#

Pros:

  • The day gets written even when you do nothing: screenshots, OCR, narrative, all automatic
  • Everything local: capture, transcription, search, knowledge graph; no account exists
  • Meetings recorded and transcribed on-device, with action items sent to Today's focus once an AI provider is set up
  • Your choice of AI provider, including fully local models, or none at all

Cons:

  • macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only; no iPhone, no iPad, no Windows, no web
  • Private beta with limited spots
  • AI prose features (narrative, Ask, Reflection) sit idle until you configure a provider
  • It records what you did, not what you felt; nobody's printing this into a gift book
  • Memory won't show "Ready" until 5 days, 300 frames, 60 things, and 3 threads accumulate
  • On a new install, a day's text and written entry are deleted with its screenshots after 30 days unless you change the setting

Pricing#

Day One, 12-month totals (checked 2026-08-04, dayoneapp.com/plans):

  • Basic, $0: unlimited entries and journals, 1 photo per entry, E2E encryption, prompts, templates, 1 device, web access
  • Silver, $49.99/yr: unlimited photos/videos (30 per entry), unlimited-device sync, audio recording + transcription, Strava/Zapier/IFTTT, email & SMS journaling
  • Gold, $74.99/yr: adds Daily Chat, AI summaries and prompts, Title Suggestions, Image Generation, Day One Labs

A 1-month trial of the paid tiers is available in-app. Gotchas: billing changes must happen on the platform where you originally subscribed, and AI features have daily limits.

Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots, and there's no public price yet.

FAQ#

Does Day One have AI?

Yes, since April 2026, on the Gold tier ($74.99/yr): Daily Chat guided reflection, reflective summaries, Go Deeper prompts, Entry Summaries, Title Suggestions, and Image Generation. Apple Intelligence features are included, and all AI features have daily usage limits. The official pages reviewed don't disclose processing for the non-Apple-Intelligence AI features.

Is Day One worth paying for?

If you journal across devices or want media-rich entries, yes: Silver at $49.99/yr is the tier where the app becomes whole (unlimited photos, sync, audio transcription). If you only write plain text on one device, Basic at $0 is genuinely usable forever. Gold is worth it only if you'll actually use Daily Chat; the usage limits mean heavy AI users may feel capped.

Is Day One end-to-end encrypted?

For journals created after v4.2 on iOS/Mac, yes, by default: AES-GCM-256, key escrowed to your own iCloud or Google Drive. Android and older journals require manual opt-in. Day One states it can't view encrypted content and will never sell journal data.

Can Everpaper replace Day One?

Only if your goal is a record of your workday on a Mac. Everpaper writes about what you did (apps, files, meetings, sites) automatically and locally. It doesn't run on your phone, doesn't hold your photos, and doesn't ask how you felt. Reflective journalers should stay with Day One (or use both).

Does Everpaper sync to my phone?

No. Your journal lives on one Mac with no account or cloud sync. That's the privacy model, and it's also the limit.

Final recommendation#

These apps share a shelf, not a market. Day One is a mature manual journal with AI-assisted reflection and a usable free tier. If writing is the point, use it for that. Everpaper is for the days you'd otherwise forget: an automatic, private, on-device day journal plus meeting recording and a second brain, for people whose lives happen on a Mac.
