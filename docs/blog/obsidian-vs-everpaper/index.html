Obsidian vs Everpaper: an honest 2026 comparison guide

Obsidian vs Everpaper, compared honestly in 2026. Free local Markdown power vs automatic on-device capture, and why many users run both apps together.

By Marco Iannello · Updated August 4, 2026

Everpaper is our product, so read this with that in mind. We'll be fair anyway: Obsidian is genuinely excellent at what it does, and for a lot of people the honest answer to "Obsidian or Everpaper?" is "both." We'll explain why below.

Quick verdict

Choose Obsidian if…

  • You want a free (including commercial use, since February 2025) note-taking app that runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android.
  • You enjoy writing and linking your own notes by hand, in plain-text Markdown files you own forever.
  • You want a plugin ecosystem (2,000+ community plugins) and don't mind configuring your setup.
  • You're on any platform other than an Apple Silicon Mac.

Choose Everpaper if…

  • You want capture to happen automatically, via periodic screenshots, OCR, and meeting transcription, instead of by typing.
  • Your notes keep not getting written because you were busy doing the thing.
  • You want meeting minutes with speaker-labeled, on-device transcripts for Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby.
  • You want a knowledge graph that builds itself from your days rather than one you link by hand.

How we compared

We compared Obsidian's published pricing and product documentation, checked 2026-08-04, with Everpaper's shipped docs. The criteria are capture, file ownership, knowledge graphs, AI behavior, privacy, platform coverage, export, and price.

Head-to-head

Capture: automatic beats manual, and it isn't close

Verdict: Everpaper, decisively. This is the core difference between the two apps.

Obsidian captures what you type. That's it. There's no meeting bot, no screen capture, no audio transcription. First-party capture beyond typing is the Web Clipper browser extension. If you didn't write it down, it isn't in your vault. Obsidian users know the failure mode well: the meeting where you were too deep in the discussion to take notes, the afternoon that vanished, the idea you were sure you'd remember.

Everpaper's Day Journal takes periodic screenshots of your front window (default every 2 minutes; 30 seconds to 10 minutes configurable), OCRs them on-device, and writes an AI day narrative. Meetings get their own pipeline: press ⇧⌘M, and Everpaper records microphone and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks and transcribes on-device with speaker labels. You do nothing. The record exists whether or not you were in a note-taking mood.

The vault itself: Obsidian wins on openness and price

Verdict: Obsidian. Plain-text Markdown files remain readable by other tools. That's a strong data-ownership model. Since 2025-02-20 the app is free for personal and commercial use, no account required, no ads, no tracking. The optional Commercial license ($50/user/year) is now a support-the-developers gesture, not a requirement.

Obsidian is also genuinely cross-platform (Windows, Linux, Android, iOS) where Everpaper is macOS 14.2+ on Apple Silicon only, with no mobile, no web, and no Windows version. If your life spans platforms, Obsidian is the only one of the two that follows you.

The graph: hand-linked vs self-built

Verdict: different philosophies. Everpaper for zero effort, Obsidian for deliberate structure.

Obsidian's graph is only as good as the links you write. That's a feature if you believe thinking is linking: the graph encodes your judgment. It's a bug if you never get around to linking, in which case the graph view is a sparse scatter of orphaned notes.

Everpaper's Second Brain builds its graph from what actually happened: people, files, sites, projects, and topics extracted from your captured days and meetings, each tied back to exact moments. There's a readiness gate (it needs 5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, and 3 recurring threads before it shows "Ready"), and beliefs form with confidence markers, decay after quiet days, and surface dropped items as open loops. You don't curate it. It watches, and it cites its evidence.

AI: who ships what

Verdict: Everpaper for integrated on-device AI; Obsidian for flexibility if you configure plugins.

Obsidian ships no first-party AI. Community plugins fill the gap: Smart Connections runs a local embedding model by default ("zero setup, no API key"), and Copilot for Obsidian sends vault context to whatever provider you configure: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or local models via Ollama or LM Studio. Whether your notes leave the device depends entirely on which plugin and provider you set up.

Everpaper's default is stricter: capture, OCR, transcription, search, the knowledge graph, and beliefs all run locally with no provider at all. AI features (Ask the Book, Second Brain chat, the day narrative) wait until you configure one, and local options (Ollama, LM Studio, oMLX, Apple Foundation Models) mean nothing has to leave the Mac. Cloud providers require flipping Allow cloud AI to analyze your journal, which is off by default, and a redaction pass scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and similar secrets before anything goes out.

Privacy: both good, different mechanisms

Verdict: tie, with different trust models. Obsidian's apps collect no telemetry and your vault is local plain text; the paid Sync add-on is AES-256 end-to-end encrypted. Everpaper keeps everything on-device by default with no account anywhere, runs speech recognition on the Neural Engine, and redacts secrets at capture time and again before every outbound AI call. With Obsidian, your exposure surface is the plugins you install; with Everpaper, it's the AI provider you choose. Both apps let you keep that surface at zero.

The part most comparisons miss: Everpaper feeds Obsidian

This is the point that changes the frame from "vs" to "and."

Everpaper has a first-class Export Obsidian vault feature. It writes your entire journal as linked Markdown: a Days/ folder with each day's narrative, timeline, and linked People, Links, and Files (with frontmatter); People/, Links/, Files/, and Topics/ folders; and a GRAPH_REPORT.md mapping the content. It's plain Markdown, there's no lock-in, and re-running the export refreshes the vault in place. See the export docs for the full layout.

What that means in practice: let Everpaper do the capturing (the days, the meetings, the automatic graph) and export to Obsidian, where the output becomes part of the vault you hand-curate, link, and publish from. Everpaper solves the "I never wrote it down" problem; Obsidian solves the "my life's work lives here" problem. They compose. The export also works with Logseq or any Markdown editor, so this isn't a funnel into either product.

Use cases

PhD student or researcher: Obsidian as home base: literature notes, Zettelkasten, hand-built links. Add Everpaper if your days include recorded seminars and meetings you want transcribed without a bot joining the call.

Founder or manager in meetings all day: Everpaper. Calendar auto-start (opt-in) detects Zoom/Meet/Teams links and offers to record with a 5-second cancellable countdown; Extract todos pulls action items into Today's Focus. Export the vault to Obsidian weekly if you keep project docs there.

Writer with a decade of Markdown: Stay in Obsidian. Consider Everpaper alongside it as the automatic memory layer; the export means your captured days land in your existing vault as ordinary files.

Windows or Android user: Obsidian. Everpaper can't run on your machine today.

Honest pros and cons

Obsidian, pros: Free for commercial use since 2025-02-20. Plain-text Markdown, zero lock-in. 2,000+ plugins. Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android. No telemetry, independent and user-funded.

Obsidian, cons: All capture is manual. Steep learning curve: vaults, links, and plugin configuration overwhelm newcomers. Mobile app draws frequent complaints about slow startup and lag on large vaults. No first-party AI, transcription, or meeting features. Official Sync's Standard plan ($4/mo annually) caps at 1 GB storage and 5 MB files, which frustrates users with big vaults; free sync via iCloud or Syncthing is fiddly.

Everpaper, pros: Automatic capture you don't have to remember to do. On-device meeting transcription with speaker labels, plus ⌥Space recall from any app. Self-building knowledge graph with evidence-linked beliefs. Exports everything as a linked Markdown vault, no lock-in. No account, no default cloud.

Everpaper, cons: macOS 14.2+ and Apple Silicon only: no Intel, Windows, Linux, mobile, or web. Private beta with limited spots. AI features wait for you to configure a provider. The Second Brain sits behind a readiness gate (5 days, 300 frames, 60 things, 3 threads) before it shows Ready. The speech model needs a few GB of disk.

Pricing

Obsidian (checked 2026-08-04): The core app is free for personal and commercial use; 12-month total: $0. Add-ons, billed annually: Sync Standard $4/user/mo ($48/year, 1 vault, 1 GB, 5 MB max file, 1-month history), Sync Plus $8/user/mo ($96/year, 10 vaults, 10 GB, 12-month history), Publish $8/site/mo ($96/year). Catalyst early access is $25 one-time. Students, faculty, and nonprofits get 40% off Sync and Publish. Many users pay nothing and sync via iCloud or git.

Everpaper: In private beta with limited spots, with a founding-license option for early supporters, not a monthly subscription. We don't publish a price, so we won't quote one here.

FAQ

Can Everpaper export to Obsidian? Yes. Export Obsidian vault writes your whole journal as a linked Markdown vault (Days/, People/, Links/, Files/, Topics/, plus a GRAPH_REPORT.md) to a local folder you can open directly in Obsidian (or Logseq, or any Markdown editor). Re-running the export refreshes it in place. Details in the export documentation.

Does Obsidian have AI features? Not first-party. Community plugins add them: Smart Connections does local embeddings-based related notes by default, and Copilot for Obsidian chats with your vault through whatever LLM provider you configure, cloud or local.

Is Obsidian really free for work? Yes. Since 2025-02-20 the commercial license requirement was dropped. Anyone can use Obsidian for work, free, with no account. The $50/user/year Commercial license still exists but is optional, for supporting development (checked 2026-08-04).

Can Everpaper replace Obsidian? For capture and recall, it can cover a different part of the workflow. As a long-term, hand-curated writing environment spanning every platform, it cannot: the supplied Everpaper docs describe no comparable editor or cross-platform vault app. Its export feature lets Obsidian remain that home.

Which is more private? Both are strong. Obsidian: local plain-text files, no telemetry, optional E2E encrypted sync. Everpaper: everything on-device by default, no account, Neural Engine transcription, redaction before any optional cloud AI call. Either can be run with zero data leaving your machine.

Final recommendation

If you can only pick one: Obsidian is the safer default, free and cross-platform, and your files outlive every app. If your real problem is that life happens faster than you can type, Everpaper is the only one of the two that captures automatically, and its Obsidian vault export means choosing it costs you nothing in lock-in.

Our actual recommendation for Mac users who care about both: run them together. Everpaper captures; Obsidian keeps.
