Reflect vs Everpaper: an honest 2026 privacy comparison

Reflect vs Everpaper compared honestly — E2EE networked notes with cloud AI versus a fully on-device Mac daybook. Privacy, capture, pricing, verdicts.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 8 Oct 2026

Reflect and Everpaper both care about privacy and get recommended in some of the same PKM discussions. They're also fundamentally different products. Reflect is a networked note-taking app: you write notes, link them, and an AI assistant helps. Everpaper is an on-device daybook: it captures what happened on your Mac and writes the record for you.

Before the verdicts: Everpaper is our product. We've tried to be fair below, including where Reflect genuinely wins. Pricing was checked on 2026-08-04 against each product's own site.

Quick verdict#

Choose Reflect if:

  • You want a fast, polished, manual note-taking app with backlinks and daily notes
  • End-to-end encrypted sync across Mac, iPhone, and iPad matters more than local AI
  • You want AI as an assistant to your own writing, not an author
  • You want a mature product (shipping since 2021) with a real iOS app today

Choose Everpaper if:

  • You want the record captured automatically: meetings and workdays, not just what you remembered to type
  • You want AI features that can run entirely on your Mac, with nothing sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google
  • You want meeting recording and speaker-labeled transcription, which Reflect doesn't do at all
  • You're on an Apple Silicon Mac and can live without iOS or Windows for now

How we compared#

We read both products' own documentation and privacy policies (reflect.academy, reflect.app/privacy, and our own docs), checked pricing pages on 2026-08-04, and compared on privacy model, capture, AI, meetings, platforms, and price.

Privacy: Reflect wins at encryption, Everpaper wins at AI#

Verdict: Reflect's E2EE is real and audited. Everpaper's local AI path is the stronger story for the AI features specifically. Different threat models.

Reflect uses client-side E2E encryption with XChaCha20-Poly1305, so notes are encrypted on your device before sync and Reflect's servers hold "an encrypted blob which we can't read." That's verbatim from their security page, backed by an independent Doyensec audit (Q2 2021, published in full) that found the "cryptographic primitives and their usage is sound." Their encryption library, kiss-crypto, is open source. If your threat model is "the note vendor gets breached," this is a strong design.

The carve-out is the AI. Use Reflect's AI palette (⌘J), chat with your notes, or transcribe a voice memo, and plaintext leaves the encryption boundary. Their security page says it directly: "text selected and processed with our AI feature is also sent to OpenAI's servers." Voice audio goes to OpenAI's Whisper API, AI chat runs on Google Gemini, and the assistant uses OpenAI or Anthropic. The privacy policy's own caveat: "OpenAI and Anthropic may use prompts we send them as training data." Reflect itself doesn't train on your data. Its AI vendors might.

Everpaper's model skips the encryption question for its core: there is no cloud. Capture, OCR, transcription, storage, search, and the knowledge graph all run on-device with no account and no sync. AI features wait for you to configure a provider, and local providers (Everpaper Native on macOS 27, oMLX, LM Studio, Ollama) mean nothing leaves the Mac. Cloud providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter) exist but sit behind a toggle, Allow cloud AI to analyze your journal, that's off by default. Before any outbound AI call, an on-device redaction pass scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs into [REDACTED:api_key]-style placeholders. Details in the privacy docs.

The honest nuance: Reflect protects against the vendor at rest and in transit; Everpaper protects by not having a vendor in the loop. Reflect's weakness is that its flagship AI features break its own encryption boundary. Everpaper's is that your data's safety depends on your Mac's physical security, and enabling a cloud AI provider puts you back in "trust the LLM vendor" territory, mitigated by redaction.

Capture: manual vs automatic#

Verdict: different philosophies, both executed well. Reflect if you want to write; Everpaper if you want the day to write itself.

Reflect is organized around a daily note you fill in yourself: typed notes with backlinks, voice memos transcribed via Whisper, a Chrome/Safari web clipper, Kindle highlights sync, and Google Calendar / Outlook event import. It's a lovely, opinionated writing surface, praised in r/PKMS threads for speed and polish. Everything in your Reflect graph is there because you put it there.

Everpaper captures what actually happened. The Day Journal takes a full-screen screenshot every 2 minutes by default (30 s to 10 min), OCRs it locally, reviews new activity every 10 minutes, and writes the day up when you stop (or, in Activity only mode, keeps an app-and-window timeline with no screenshots). Screenshots are kept 30 days by default, then pruned; on a new install, the day's text and written entry expire with them unless you choose Forever or turn off Also delete their text & recall data. Meetings aren't affected. A Mark Moment button pins anything with an optional note, and pause reasons (Meeting / Break / Lunch / Personal / custom) keep private stretches private. At day's end there's a review you can ask about. More in the Day Journal docs.

Neither is wrong. Reflect's model produces notes you thought about; Everpaper's produces a record of what you did, including the parts you'd never have written down. If you've ever opened a journal app at 10pm unable to reconstruct your own Tuesday, that's the gap Everpaper closes.

AI: assistant vs author, cloud vs local#

Verdict: Reflect's AI palette is more polished today. Everpaper is the only one whose AI can run with zero network traffic.

Reflect's AI is well-designed. The ⌘J palette transforms highlighted text with GPT-4o or Claude 3.5 Sonnet (toggleable in preferences), ships ready-made prompts plus custom prompts, and the chat-with-your-notes search is a real time-saver. Usage caps are 100k tokens/day on paid accounts, 10k/day on trial, and you can bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic API key to bypass the caps. There's even an MCP server (beta) so Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor can read and write your Reflect notes.

Everpaper's AI works differently. Without any provider configured, you still get capture, OCR, search, deterministic recall cards (phone numbers, emails, links, files with time-and-place stamps), and locally computed beliefs and open loops in Memory (also called Second Brain). Configure a provider and the prose features wake up: Ask your memory… (⇧⌘A) streams answers written only from matching captured frames with clickable sources, plus the end-of-day review chat, the Reflection, and meeting Ask. Point it at a local model and the whole AI stack stays on the machine. Guided setup picks a model for your Mac: Everpaper Native on macOS 27 (Gemma 4 E4B on 16 GB Macs), or an oMLX server Everpaper installs with Gemma 4 E4B on 24 GB+ Macs and a smaller model below. Speech runs on-device: Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) writes the words and Nemotron labels speakers by default, Whisper is an option, and Detailed meetings re-listen with MOSS.

One caveat in each direction. Reflect's AI works on day one with zero setup; Everpaper makes you configure a provider first, and Memory has a readiness gate before it's fully useful. The Memory guide explains the readiness thresholds and what still works before the gate opens.

Meetings: not close#

Verdict: Everpaper, by default. Reflect doesn't record meetings at all.

Reflect handles meetings the manual way: import the calendar event, open the daily note, take notes yourself, optionally use the AI palette to summarize what you typed. There is no meeting bot and no call recording.

Everpaper records the meeting itself: microphone plus system audio as separate time-aligned tracks, started with ⇧⌘M or Index (⌘K) → Begin meeting. It recognizes Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby links, transcribes on-device with speaker labels, and the live panel gives you your notes with an "As we go" digest, a live transcript, and Ask over what's been said. More → Extract To-Dos into Today sends action items to Today's focus, and the Speakers tab (⌘4) can hide a voice from that meeting's transcript view (the audio and exports keep everyone). An opt-in Calendar Auto-Start can start recording automatically with a 5-second cancellable countdown; calendar events never leave the Mac. No bot joins your call. Details in the meetings docs.

Platforms: Reflect's clearest win#

Verdict: Reflect, decisively. Everpaper is Mac-only.

Reflect runs on macOS (both Apple Silicon and Intel builds), iPhone, iPad (TestFlight), the web, and Chrome/Safari extensions, works offline, and syncs encrypted when reconnected. That's a real, daily-usable cross-device product. No Windows or Android app, a recurring dealbreaker in PKM threads, but inside the Apple world it's everywhere.

Everpaper is macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon only (its speech and AI models are built for Apple silicon; Intel Macs are unsupported). No iOS, no iPad, no web, no Windows. It's also a private beta with limited spots. If notes on a phone are part of your day, Reflect is the only option of the two.

Which one fits your use case?#

Use Reflect when writing, linking, and reading notes across Apple devices is the job. Use Everpaper when you need to reconstruct a workday or meeting without relying on what you remembered to type.

Honest pros and cons#

Reflect#

Pros

  • True client-side E2EE with XChaCha20-Poly1305, backed by a published Doyensec audit and an open-source encryption library
  • Fast, minimalist daily-notes UX with backlinks; the community praise is consistent
  • iOS, iPad, web, and browser extensions; works offline, syncs when reconnected
  • Polished AI palette with bring-your-own-key option, plus a forward-looking MCP server
  • Indie, bootstrapped team (~4 people) shipping steadily since 2021

Cons

  • Every AI feature sends plaintext to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, and its privacy policy admits those providers "may use prompts we send them as training data"
  • $10/month billed annually only: no free tier, and $120 up front
  • No meeting recording; voice memos are transcribed with OpenAI Whisper
  • Lose your password, lose your notes permanently; their security page says so plainly
  • Closed-source client, and they admit "ultimately you have to trust the client, and the client can change"

Everpaper#

Pros

  • Fully on-device by default: capture, OCR, transcription, storage, search, and knowledge graph run offline after a one-time speech-model download, no account anywhere
  • AI features can run entirely on local providers (Everpaper Native on macOS 27, oMLX, LM Studio, Ollama); "no provider, no traffic"
  • Automatic capture of the workday plus real meeting recording with speaker labels and on-device transcription
  • On-device redaction of secrets before every outbound AI call; Memory's answers are checked against their sources, and unsupported sentences are removed (the check isn't perfect, so the sources are shown)
  • Export to a plain-Markdown Obsidian vault plus GraphML/Mermaid graph, no lock-in (see the export docs)

Cons

  • macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only: no iOS, no iPad, no web, no Windows, no Intel
  • Private beta with limited spots; AI prose features wait until you configure a provider
  • Memory has a readiness gate (5 days, 300 frames, 60 things, 3 threads) before it reads Ready
  • The documented workflow centers on captured days and meetings; Reflect remains the deliberate writing tool in this comparison

Pricing#

Reflect (checked 2026-08-04, reflect.app): one plan, one price: $10/month billed annually, so $120 for 12 months. That includes everything: networked notes, AI assistant (within the 100k tokens/day cap), E2EE encryption, calendar integrations, sync, Kindle highlights, web clipper. There's a 14-day free trial and no permanent free tier. A month-to-month option is not listed on the current site.

Everpaper: in private beta with limited spots. We don't publish a price during beta.

FAQ#

Is Reflect really private?#

Reflect has a strong cloud-note privacy model with one specific carve-out. The notes themselves are end-to-end encrypted: XChaCha20-Poly1305, encrypted on your device before sync, Doyensec-audited in 2021, with the encryption library open-sourced. Reflect's servers store blobs they can't read.

The hole is the AI layer. Highlight text and hit ⌘J, chat with your notes, or transcribe a voice memo, and that content goes to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google in plaintext. Reflect's own security page confirms "text selected and processed with our AI feature is also sent to OpenAI's servers," and its privacy policy states those providers "may use prompts we send them as training data." You can skip AI features entirely and keep the full E2EE guarantee. So: yes, Reflect is really private for storage and sync; no, its AI features are not covered by that encryption. That's a trade-off they document honestly.

Does Reflect work offline?#

Yes. Notes work offline on mobile and desktop and sync encrypted blobs when you reconnect. Everpaper also works fully offline by default; even its AI features run offline with a local provider.

Can Everpaper replace Reflect?#

Only if what you wanted from Reflect is the record rather than its writing experience. Everpaper captures your day and meetings automatically and lets you search or chat with that history locally. Reflect handles daily notes, web clips, backlinks, and writing your own thinking. Everpaper also has no iOS app. For a wider field, see our Reflect alternatives roundup.

Does Reflect have a free plan?#

No. There's a 14-day free trial, then one plan: $10/month billed annually ($120/year), checked 2026-08-04.

Which is better for meetings?#

Everpaper, because Reflect doesn't record or transcribe meetings at all; it imports calendar events for manual notes. Everpaper records mic and system audio locally, transcribes on-device with speaker labels, and, with an AI provider set up, puts a finished meeting's action items in Today's focus.

Does Everpaper sync to my phone like Reflect does?#

No. There's no cloud sync, no account, and no mobile or web version; your data stays on the Mac. Reflect's E2EE sync across Mac, iPhone, and iPad is one of its best features.

Final recommendation#

Stay with Reflect if#

These two products only look like competitors. Reflect answers "where do I write my thinking so it's encrypted, linked, and available on my phone?" Its E2EE details are documented and audited, and the app works across Apple's platforms. If that's your question, Reflect fits. Just know that its AI features step outside the encryption boundary and send selected content to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google.

Choose Everpaper if#

Everpaper answers a different question: "what actually happened today, and can I ask about it without any of it leaving my Mac?" If you want the day and meetings captured automatically, transcribed on-device, and queried through AI that can run on local models, that's the bet we're making. The limits are macOS-only, Apple Silicon-only, and private beta availability.

Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots. Apply for the private beta; if you're selected, you'll hear back within 48 hours. Want the privacy model in detail first? Read the privacy docs.
