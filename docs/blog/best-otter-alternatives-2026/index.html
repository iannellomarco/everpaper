4 Best Otter.ai Alternatives for 2026

Compare four top Otter.ai alternatives for privacy, bot-free capture, accuracy, and price, including options for Apple Silicon Macs.

By Marco Iannello · Updated August 4, 2026

Looking for the best Otter.ai alternatives in 2026? The right choice depends on what you are trying to escape: a meeting bot, cloud processing, transcription caps, or a transcript that becomes another silo. For privacy-first users on supported Apple Silicon Macs, Everpaper is our #1 Otter alternative: its local capture path addresses bot, cloud-processing, and quota concerns, while its broader daybook workflow addresses transcript isolation. Granola is the best bot-free cloud option, Fireflies.ai is the closest feature match, and ScreenPipe is the most flexible local toolkit for developers. Every pick below includes dated pricing and honest drawbacks.

Why people are looking for an Otter alternative

Otter.ai is the default meeting notetaker for a lot of teams, and it earned that spot with mature live transcription and broad platform coverage. But three things have pushed managers to reevaluate in 2026.

The bot problem. Otter's "OtterPilot" (also called Otter Notetaker) joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams call as a visible meeting participant, auto-joining through calendar sync. It records the audio, transcribes in real time, captures slides, and emails a summary afterward. A 2022 Hacker News report captured the failure mode that still circulates: "The bot proceeded to join two confidential meetings on my behalf and record the whole thing, then email every member an absurd, inaccurate 'outline.'" Reddit's r/projectmanagement has a thread titled "Do not join Otter.Ai unless you want your whole company spammed." For external calls, client meetings, and anything sensitive, a visible recording bot changes the conversation.

The free tier squeeze. Back in August 2022 Otter cut the free plan from 600 monthly transcription minutes to 300, with a 30-minute cap per conversation and only 3 lifetime audio/video file imports (checked 2026-08-04 on otter.ai/pricing and Otter's help center). Deleted conversations still count toward the monthly limit. Capterra reviewers complain about the minute-tracking overhead: "Having to track usage and stay within a set number of transcription minutes added unnecessary stress."

The lawsuit. Four class actions filed between August and September 2025 (the first was Brewer v. Otter.ai) were consolidated as In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation in the Northern District of California, alleging ECPA, CIPA, and BIPA violations: recording without all-party consent and using recordings to train AI. The motion-to-dismiss hearing was held on 2026-05-20 before Judge Eumi K. Lee. Separately, Otter's own privacy policy (effective June 16, 2026) reserves the right to train "proprietary AI technology on de-identified audio recordings and on transcriptions (which may contain Personal Information)," and discloses data-labeling providers who annotate user content. Otter claimed 35M+ users as of December 2025, so this isn't a niche concern.

None of that makes Otter a bad product. It makes it a product whose trade-offs (a visible bot, cloud storage on AWS, training on your conversations, minute caps) some teams no longer want to accept. Here's what we'd switch to.

How we evaluated

We scored each Otter alternative on five criteria, weighted for the audience reading this page (managers and teams reevaluating after privacy news):

  • Consent posture: does a bot join the call, or does capture happen on your own machine?
  • Data residency: cloud upload by default, or on-device by default? Who trains on your audio?
  • Transcription quality: engine, speaker labeling, accuracy in real meetings.
  • Price honesty: actual entry cost with a check date, including free-tier limits.
  • Fit beyond meetings: does the tool do anything with the rest of your workday, or is it a transcript silo?

Every competitor price below carries a "checked 2026-08-04" stamp from our research files. Where a fact couldn't be verified, we say so inline.

1. Everpaper: best for privacy-first users on supported Apple Silicon Macs

Overview. Everpaper is a private, on-device daybook for macOS. Its meeting feature, The Minutes, records microphone and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks and transcribes everything on your Mac; the speech model runs on the Apple Neural Engine. No bot joins the call. No account exists. Nothing is uploaded by default, and the privacy story is the product: "No provider, no traffic." AI features wait until you configure one.

What that means in practice.

  • Capture does not add a bot to the meeting: start with ⇧⌘M, the Record tile, the menu bar, or Index (⌘K) → Begin minutes. Everpaper recognizes Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby links. You remain responsible for any recording consent your jurisdiction requires.
  • Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) runs on-device by default, so recording and transcription are not metered against cloud minutes. Whisper and MOSS are alternatives; MOSS combines transcription and diarization in one opt-in model.
  • The floating panel shows a live, speaker-labeled transcript while you work. Hide my voice / Hide any speaker (⌘4) persists per meeting, so you can exclude a voice without redoing the setting every time.
  • Offline redaction protects sensitive strings during capture and runs again before any outbound AI call. API keys, JWTs, card numbers, SSNs, and OTPs become [REDACTED:api_key]-style placeholders. See how the privacy model works.
  • A meeting stays connected to the rest of your day: Extract todos sends action items into Today's Focus, and the Second Brain builds a local knowledge graph across days. Import Transcript also ingests .vtt files from Zoom, Teams, and Otter as real meetings rather than loose reference files.
  • Optional calendar auto-start uses a 5-second cancellable countdown, reducing missed recordings without handing calendar control to a cloud bot. Calendar data is read-only, local, and never uploaded. More in the meetings docs.

Drawbacks. Everpaper is macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only: no Intel Macs, Windows, mobile, or web app. It is in private beta, so your whole team cannot sign up today, and it is not yet a mature shared team-collaboration workspace. Teams that need a cross-platform rollout and established collaboration features should choose another option in this list. The Second Brain has a readiness gate (5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, 3 recurring threads) before it says "Ready." AI features like Ask and summaries wait for a provider you configure (local options include Ollama, LM Studio, oMLX, and Apple Foundation Models; cloud providers are off by default behind an explicit toggle). MOSS is happiest on 16 GB machines.

Pricing. In private beta with limited spots, with a founding-license option for early supporters, not a monthly subscription. No public price; join the waitlist.

Best for: individual managers and teams centered on Apple Silicon Macs who want Otter's core job (record, transcribe, summarize, extract action items) without a bot, an account, a cloud upload by default, or monthly transcription-minute anxiety—and who can accept private-beta access and limited team collaboration today.

Moving VTT transcripts from Otter to Everpaper

  1. Export the Otter conversations you want to keep as .vtt files.
  2. In Everpaper, choose Import Transcript and select those exports.
  3. Everpaper turns each .vtt into a real meeting that is searchable and linked to your journal; no audio file is required.
  4. Record new meetings with The Minutes so future transcripts, action items, and daybook context stay together on your Mac.

This migrates the .vtt transcript history you export from Otter, not your full Otter history or archive.

2. Granola: best bot-free cloud option

Overview. Granola is an AI meeting notepad that captures microphone plus system audio locally, then streams audio to transcription providers (Deepgram, AssemblyAI) and sends text to OpenAI and Anthropic for notes. There is no meeting bot; it runs on your computer and works with anything playing through system audio, including in-person meetings via the mobile apps.

What we like.

  • Note quality is widely praised. Nat Friedman (an investor via NFDG): "it generates the best meeting notes of anything I've tried so far." The human-in-the-loop model, where you jot rough notes and AI fleshes them out from the transcript, gives more control than fully auto summaries.
  • Bot-free by design, and manual by default: Granola doesn't transcribe unless you've clicked into a note. Works on macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android, the widest platform coverage of any bot-free option here.
  • Audio is not stored: "It only stores the transcript and any notes," hosted in a US AWS VPC, encrypted at rest and in transit. SOC 2 Type 2 certified, with public security post-mortems.
  • Business tier adds integrations with Attio, Notion, Slack, HubSpot, Affinity, Zapier, plus MCP and API access.

Drawbacks. It's bot-free, not on-device. Audio still goes to Deepgram/AssemblyAI and text to OpenAI/Anthropic, and Granola trains its own models on anonymized data by default on consumer tiers (opt-out in Settings; off by default on Enterprise). Speaker identification is weak: transcripts default to "Me" and "Them" labels, with named speaker tags only for Google Meet (browser extension) and Zoom on macOS. No recording playback ever, so you can't verify the transcript against audio. No file import. Sign-in requires a Google or Microsoft account.

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04, granola.ai/pricing). Basic is free with limited meeting history; the official pricing page does not state the exact cap. Business is $14 per user/month. Enterprise is $35 per user/month with SSO, org-wide auto-deletion, and training off for the team.

Best for: teams who hate the bot but are fine with cloud processing, especially mixed Mac/Windows shops.

3. Fireflies.ai: closest Otter feature match

Overview. Fireflies is the most direct like-for-like Otter replacement: a bot ("Fred") joins Zoom, Meet, Teams, and Webex calls as a participant, transcribes in 100+ languages, and layers on summaries, action items, conversation intelligence, and team analytics.

What we like.

  • The free tier is genuinely usable: unlimited transcription and AI summaries (rate-limited), 400 minutes of storage per team, 20 AI credits, API access on every tier (checked 2026-08-04, fireflies.ai/pricing).
  • Their security page states plainly: "You own your data. We don't train on it by default unlike other AI companies," with 256-bit AES at rest, TLS in transit, and a 0-day retention policy with vendors. SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA via BAA on Enterprise.
  • Pro and higher tiers include unlimited integrations, while every tier includes API access and Fireflies documents a GraphQL API.
  • Enterprise offers private storage, custom data retention, and a transcript-and-summary-only mode.

Drawbacks. The bot problem is the same as Otter's: Fred appears in the participant list, calendar auto-join is on by default, and users report it's hard to fully disable. And honesty requires this: Fireflies faces its own biometric-privacy class action, Cruz v. Fireflies.AI Corp., filed December 2025 in the Central District of Illinois, alleging its speaker-recognition feature collected and stored voiceprints without BIPA-required notice and written consent. It's ongoing with no settlement as of 2026-08-04. Accuracy struggles with accents and crosstalk, and Trustpilot reviews complain about AI-credit billing surprises. None of its AI features work offline.

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04, fireflies.ai/pricing). Free $0. Pro $10 per seat/month billed annually ($18 monthly). Business $19 per seat/month billed annually ($29 monthly). Enterprise $39 per seat/month, annual only.

Best for: teams that want Otter's exact workflow (bot, integrations, analytics) with a stronger free tier and a no-training-by-default policy, and who accept the same consent questions that come with any meeting bot.

4. ScreenPipe: best for developers who want 24/7 local memory

Overview. ScreenPipe is a local-first 24/7 screen and microphone recorder for macOS, Windows, and Linux, written in Rust. It captures screenshots and accessibility-tree text continuously (default 1 FPS), transcribes with local Whisper (whisper-tiny through whisper-large-v3-turbo), and exposes everything through a local SQLite database and HTTP API at localhost:3030. No bot; it records whatever your machine displays and hears, so it works with any meeting app.

What we like.

  • Genuinely local-first: data lives in ~/.screenpipe/db.sqlite, works fully offline for capture, OCR, transcription, and search. Default-on PII removal redacts emails, phone numbers, and card numbers; homepage claims password managers are excluded and cards and keys redacted.
  • Developer surface: local API, an MCP server for Claude, Codex, Cursor, and ChatGPT, and "pipes," scheduled agents that can, say, summarize a meeting when it ends and drop a note into Obsidian.
  • Free entry: npx -y screenpipe@latest record gives you local recording, search, and the API at no cost. Cross-platform including Linux CLI, the broadest coverage on this list.
  • Y Combinator backed (S26), auditable source.

Drawbacks. This is infrastructure, not a finished notetaker; you assemble your own meeting workflow from pipes and prompts. The official documentation reviewed does not state whether speaker identification is supported. The license moved from MIT to the Screenpipe Commercial License on 2026-06-09 (source-available; any commercial use of the source needs a paid license), and the business model has confused users enough to spawn an Ask HN thread. Community marketing was aggressive enough that r/LocalLLaMA had a ban-request thread. Official docs acknowledge CPU and battery concerns, advising users to lower capture FPS on older machines.

Pricing (checked 2026-08-04, supplied ScreenPipe pricing page). Free is $0 with 10 AI credits/month. Basic is $25/month or $250/year with 150 credits and unlimited user-installed scheduled tasks and history. Business is $50/seat/month or $500/seat/year with 400 credits, frontier models, cloud sync, automations, and team workspace.

Best for: technical founders and developers who want a local, scriptable memory layer and are happy to build the last mile themselves.

How to choose

Answer these questions in order and the right pick usually falls out:

  1. Is a visible bot in the participant list acceptable? If no, eliminate Fireflies. You're choosing among Everpaper, Granola, and ScreenPipe.
  2. May meeting audio leave the machine at all? If legal, compliance, or your own policy says no, the field narrows to Everpaper (macOS, Apple Silicon) and ScreenPipe (any desktop OS, bring your own assembly). Granola streams audio to Deepgram or AssemblyAI and text to OpenAI or Anthropic; Fireflies is fully cloud.
  3. What's the hardware? Mixed Mac/Windows fleet rules out Everpaper. Linux in the mix points at ScreenPipe's CLI. iPhone-heavy in-person meetings point at Granola.
  4. Who maintains it? If nobody on the team will write a pipe or configure a provider, skip ScreenPipe. Granola and Fireflies offer finished cloud products instead.
  5. Do you need a mature shared team workspace today? Everpaper's private beta is not ready for that rollout. Granola or Fireflies is a better match when established team collaboration and broad access matter more than an on-device-only architecture.

FAQ

Is Otter.ai safe to use? Otter is a mature cloud SaaS. The concern here is architectural: your audio and transcripts live on Otter's AWS infrastructure, the privacy policy (June 16, 2026) reserves the right to train proprietary AI on de-identified recordings and transcripts, and data-labeling providers may annotate content. If your threat model is "we don't want our conversations stored or trained on by a vendor," Otter's architecture can't satisfy it; an on-device tool can.

Does Otter record meetings without consent? Otter's bot joins as a visible participant, so attendees can see it. But visibility isn't the same as all-party consent, and that's the heart of In re Otter.AI Privacy Litigation (N.D. Cal., MTD hearing 2026-05-20), which alleges ECPA/CIPA/BIPA violations for recording without all-party consent. Calendar auto-join means the bot can appear in meetings you forgot it was scheduled for. In two-party-consent states and countries, the host enabling Otter doesn't automatically cover every participant.

What is the best free Otter alternative? For pure free-tier generosity, Fireflies: unlimited transcription with 400 minutes of team storage (checked 2026-08-04). For bot-free capture, Granola's free Basic plan, though meeting history is limited. For local recording at zero cost, ScreenPipe's CLI is free. Everpaper isn't free; it's in private beta with a founding-license model. But nothing on its capture path ever leaves your Mac.

Can I move my Otter transcripts to another tool? Yes. Export the conversations you want to keep from Otter as .vtt files, then use Everpaper's Import Transcript feature (v1.11.0). Everpaper turns each export into a real meeting that is searchable and linked to your journal; no audio is required. Fireflies and Granola don't offer comparable Otter import paths that we've verified.

What's the best Otter alternative without a bot? Granola for cloud convenience across Mac and Windows; Everpaper for on-device privacy on Apple Silicon Macs; ScreenPipe if you want local capture on any OS and can assemble your own workflow.

Do any Otter alternatives work offline? Everpaper records, transcribes (Parakeet on the Neural Engine), and searches fully offline; AI chat features wait for a provider, and local providers like Ollama or Apple Foundation Models keep even that on-device. ScreenPipe's capture, OCR, Whisper transcription, and search all work offline. Otter, Granola, and Fireflies all require connectivity for transcription.
