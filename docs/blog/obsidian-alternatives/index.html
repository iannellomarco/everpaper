Obsidian alternatives: 3 honest options for 2026

Three Obsidian alternatives for automatic capture, cloud AI, or polished daily notes, plus the tool that exports straight into your Obsidian vault.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 8 Oct 2026

Obsidian is the best free note-taking app ever shipped, and we mean that without a hedge. It's free for personal and commercial use since February 2025, your notes are plain Markdown files, and 2,000+ community plugins cover almost any workflow. So why does "Obsidian alternatives" pull steady search volume? Because a large group of people install Obsidian, admire it, and quietly stop opening it. If that's you, this page is for you. Everpaper is our product, and it's on the list below, with our bias declared up front, its real limits stated, and one twist that changes the whole frame: Everpaper doesn't replace Obsidian. It exports an Obsidian vault. Keep reading.

Why people leave Obsidian#

From Obsidian's own forum threads, r/ObsidianMD complaint posts, and years of watching smart people abandon vaults, the same five reasons come up.

The graph only knows what you typed. Obsidian captures nothing. No meeting bot, no screen capture, no transcription. First-party capture beyond your keyboard is the Web Clipper extension. If you were too busy doing the thing to write about the thing, the vault stays empty. The much-loved graph view is a map of your note-taking discipline, not your life. People who realize this late feel cheated. The tool never promised otherwise, but the marketing-adjacent hype did.

The plugin tax. A fresh Obsidian install is deliberately minimal. Daily notes, tasks, calendar, spaced repetition, AI chat: each is a community plugin to evaluate, install, configure, and periodically debug when an update breaks it. Some users love this. Many discover they've built a second job: maintaining their note system instead of using it. r/ObsidianMD's recurring "What's bad about Obsidian?" threads put the learning curve and configuration burden at the top of the list.

No native AI. In 2026, Obsidian ships zero first-party AI. Want to chat with your vault? Install Smart Connections (local embeddings by default, genuinely good) or Copilot for Obsidian (which sends your note context to whatever LLM you configure, cloud or local). It works, but "works after I pick and wire up a third-party plugin and a provider and an API key" is a different product than "works."

Sync costs money or patience. Obsidian Sync Standard is $4/user/month billed annually ($5 monthly) for 1 synced vault, 1 GB storage, 5 MB max file size, and 1-month version history (checked 2026-08-04). Users with large vaults hit those caps fast; the Obsidian forum has threads from people who can't fit a single vault into the Standard plan. Plus ($8/month annual, 10 GB) fixes the caps at double the price. The free routes (iCloud, Syncthing, git) work but are fiddly or platform-locked, and iCloud sync conflicts are their own genre of forum post.

The learning curve never fully flattens. Markdown, vaults, wikilinks, frontmatter, templates, hotkeys. Obsidian rewards investment like few tools do, and plenty of people make that investment happily. But a real cohort bounces off in week one and feels dumb about it. They shouldn't: the tool asks a lot before it gives anything back.

If none of this stings, stop reading and stay with Obsidian. If it does, here are the honest options.

1. Everpaper: best for automatic capture that feeds your vault#

This entry has an unusual shape: Everpaper's headline feature for Obsidian users is that it writes an Obsidian vault.

Obsidian's failure mode is empty days: the meeting where you were too deep in discussion to type, the afternoon that vanished, the idea you were sure you'd remember. Everpaper fixes the capture side. Its Day Journal takes a full-screen screenshot every 2 minutes by default (30 seconds to 10 minutes) and OCRs it on-device (or, in Activity only mode, keeps an app-and-window timeline with no screenshots). After you configure an AI provider, it can write the day narrative. Meetings get their own pipeline: press ⇧⌘M, and it records mic and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks, transcribing on-device with speaker labels for Zoom, Meet, Teams, Webex, Around, and Whereby calls. Memory (also called Second Brain) builds a knowledge graph from what actually happened (people, files, sites, projects, topics, each tied to exact moments) instead of from links you remembered to write.

Then the twist: Export Obsidian vault writes your whole journal as linked Markdown: a Days/ folder with narratives, timelines, and frontmatter; People/, Links/, Files/, and Topics/ folders; plus a GRAPH_REPORT.md mapping the content. Plain Markdown, no lock-in, re-run refreshes in place. Open that folder as an Obsidian vault and your captured days sit inside the system you already curate. The export docs show the exact layout. So Everpaper is an Obsidian alternative only if your problem is Obsidian itself; if your problem is feeding Obsidian, it's the missing half.

The honest limits: macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only; no Intel, no Windows, no mobile, no web. Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots. AI features wait until you configure a provider (local options include Everpaper Native on macOS 27, oMLX, LM Studio and Ollama, so nothing has to leave the Mac). Memory sits behind a readiness gate (5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, 3 recurring threads) before it shows "Ready." More detail in Obsidian vs Everpaper.

Best for: Mac users whose vaults are empty because life happens faster than they type.

2. Mem: best for zero-organization cloud AI notes#

Mem's bet is the opposite of Obsidian's: stop organizing entirely. You dump notes in, and Mem's AI (semantic search, automatic tagging, the Mem Agent chat) finds and connects them later. No folders, no links, no graph to maintain. For people who left Obsidian because the upkeep felt like homework, that pitch lands hard.

Reviewers consistently praise the capture-and-find-later loop, and Mem Agent is among the better chat-with-your-notes implementations. The Proactive tier ($99/month after a 7-day trial, checked 2026-08-04) goes further: it tracks decisions and open loops, briefs you before meetings, and resurfaces notes with context, the closest thing to a self-maintaining vault on this list.

Now the trade-offs, and they're big. Mem is cloud-only with no offline mode, and its own pricing FAQ states data is encrypted in transit and at rest "but it is not e2e encrypted... because our core focus is to provide you with intelligence on top of your Mem." Mem's servers can read your notes. Sign-in requires a Google or Google Workspace account. The free tier (25 notes, 25 agent messages, 25 PDF pages a month) is a trial in practice. And r/PKMS has recurring threads about slow development and a weaker editor than Obsidian's. Pricing checked 2026-08-04: Pro is $12/month with no annual discount; Proactive is $99/month. Head-to-head: Mem vs Everpaper.

Best for: people who hated organizing in Obsidian and don't mind a readable-by-the-vendor cloud.

3. Reflect: best for polished daily notes with built-in AI#

Reflect is what you get when a small indie team rebuilds networked notes as an opinionated, finished product: daily notes, backlinks, calendar integration, voice memos transcribed via Whisper, and an AI palette (⌘J) with GPT-4o or Claude for transforming and chatting with your notes. No plugins to assemble; the features Obsidian users bolt on are just there.

The privacy story is real with a carve-out. Notes sync end-to-end encrypted (XChaCha20-Poly1305, independently audited by Doyensec in 2021), so Reflect's servers hold encrypted blobs they can't read. But the AI features break that boundary: highlighted text goes to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google, and Reflect's own privacy policy warns those providers "may use prompts we send them as training data." Paid accounts get 100k AI tokens/day; bring your own API key to bypass.

The catches: one plan at $10/month billed annually ($120/year, checked 2026-08-04) with no permanent free tier, only a 14-day trial. No Windows or Android app; Mac, iPhone, iPad (TestFlight beta), and web only. Lose your password and your notes are unrecoverable, by design. More in Reflect vs Everpaper.

Best for: Apple-ecosystem users who want Obsidian's backlinks without Obsidian's assembly, and can accept cloud AI.

How to choose#

Four questions, in order.

  1. Did you abandon your vault, or did you abandon writing? If the vault is thriving and you want a more opinionated shape, Reflect is the verified option here. If the vault is a graveyard of good intentions, your problem is capture, and automatic capture (Everpaper) or zero-organization AI (Mem) addresses it.
  2. Who may read your notes? Local files and on-device processing (Obsidian and Everpaper) keep the core record on your machine. E2EE sync (Reflect) means the vendor can't read notes, but its AI providers can process selected content. Mem's cloud is vendor-readable by design.
  3. What platforms do you live on? Windows or Android rules out Everpaper and Reflect. Obsidian runs across desktop and mobile. Mem is cloud-only.
  4. What's your exit format? Plain Markdown files survive any company's demise. Obsidian stores them natively, Everpaper exports a linked vault, and Mem offers a Markdown export.

FAQ#

Is there a free Obsidian alternative?#

Obsidian itself is free, including commercial use since 2025-02-20. Mem has a free tier capped at 25 notes and 25 agent messages a month, effectively a trial. Reflect has a 14-day trial but no permanent free tier. Everpaper is in private beta and has no public price yet.

What replaces Obsidian's graph view automatically?#

Two honest answers. Mem auto-organizes with AI but shows you less of the structure. Everpaper's Memory (also called Second Brain) builds a graph of people, files, sites, projects, and topics from your captured days and meetings, each entity tied back to exact moments as evidence. You can export the graph as GraphML or Mermaid for yEd, Gephi, or Obsidian's own graph plugins.

Can I keep Obsidian and still use an alternative?#

With Everpaper, that's the intended setup. Its Export Obsidian vault feature writes your captured journal as linked Markdown (Days/, People/, Links/, Files/, Topics/, GRAPH_REPORT.md) into a folder you open in Obsidian, so Everpaper captures and Obsidian keeps. Mem also exports Markdown. See the export documentation.

Which Obsidian alternative has real AI?#

Mem (cloud-only Mem Agent), Reflect (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini via the AI palette), and Everpaper have documented AI paths. Everpaper's core capture stays on-device, while AI waits for a provider you configure; local options include Everpaper Native (macOS 27), oMLX, LM Studio and Ollama. Obsidian's AI comes from community plugins like Smart Connections and Copilot, which you configure yourself.

Do any alternatives record meetings?#

Everpaper does: ⇧⌘M starts recording mic and system audio, transcribed on-device with speaker labels, with no bot joining the call. Mem's Meeting Briefings prepare you before meetings but record nothing. Reflect has no meeting capture.

Our recommendation#

Stay with Obsidian if your vault is alive and growing. It's free including commercial use, it runs on everything, 2,000+ plugins cover nearly any workflow, and your plain-text files will outlive every app on this page. The learning curve is real, but the people who climb it get a tool for life. Adding Sync Standard ($48/year) or free iCloud/git sync solves multi-device for most.

Leave for Everpaper if you're on an Apple Silicon Mac and your real problem is that nothing gets written down. Automatic capture (screenshots, OCR, meeting transcription) plus a self-building graph, all on-device, with an Obsidian vault export that means you never actually have to choose. That's the honest headline: the best Obsidian alternative might be the thing that finally fills your Obsidian vault.

Leave for Mem or Reflect if you want AI over your notes without configuring anything, and can accept their privacy trade-offs: vendor-readable cloud for Mem, cloud AI inside an E2EE shell for Reflect.
