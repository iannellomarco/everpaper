Granola vs Everpaper: an honest comparison (2026)

Granola vs Everpaper in 2026: both bot-free, but Granola streams audio to the cloud while Everpaper transcribes on-device. Full comparison.

By Marco Iannello · Updated 8 Oct 2026

Bias declared, once: Everpaper is our product. We build it, and we have an obvious interest in how this comparison lands. So we'll name Granola's real strengths plainly, including the places where it beats us, and let the data path decide the rest.

This is the fairest fight in our whole comparison series. Most AI meeting tools send a visible bot into your call; Granola and Everpaper both capture audio locally, no bot, no awkward participant-slot guest.

Quick verdict#

Choose Granola if:

  • You want the best-written AI meeting notes in the category, full stop; its note quality is the most praised we found
  • You're on Windows, or you need iPhone/Android apps for in-person meetings
  • You like jotting rough notes yourself and having AI flesh them out
  • You need team billing, Slack/HubSpot/Notion integrations, or an admin console

Choose Everpaper if:

  • Meeting audio leaving your machine is a hard no; Everpaper transcribes on your Mac, nothing streams anywhere
  • You want a day journal and a second brain alongside meetings, not meetings alone
  • You need real speaker labels and the actual audio kept
  • You don't want an account: Everpaper has no sign-in at all

Head-to-head#

Where the audio goes#

Verdict: Everpaper, and it's not close. This is the whole comparison.

Granola's no-bot design is genuinely good: the desktop app captures your microphone and system audio locally, and never transcribes a meeting you didn't click into. But then the audio streams to Deepgram or AssemblyAI for transcription, and the text goes to OpenAI or Anthropic for notes. Transcripts and notes live in Granola's US-hosted AWS, encrypted at rest and in transit. Granola is SOC 2 Type 2 certified and publishes public security post-mortems; as cloud data handling goes, it's careful and transparent.

It's still cloud. Granola also trains its own models on anonymized user data by default on the free and Business tiers; you can opt out in Settings, and training is off by default on Enterprise. To their credit, OpenAI and Anthropic are barred from training on what Granola sends them.

Everpaper's meeting pillar, Meetings, transcribes on-device: Parakeet (parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) writes the words and Nemotron labels speakers by default, Whisper is an option, and Detailed meetings re-listen with MOSS (transcription and speaker separation in one model, Apache 2.0). No account, no sign-in, no stream. The AI features (meeting Ask, summaries, the day's written entry) wait until you configure a provider, and the provider can be local: Everpaper Native (macOS 27), oMLX, LM Studio or Ollama. Cloud providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter) only work after you flip Allow cloud AI to analyze your journal, which is off by default. An offline redaction pass scrubs API keys, tokens, card numbers, and SSNs at capture time and again before any outbound AI call.

If your threat model includes "vendor gets subpoenaed" or "vendor gets acquired," architecture beats policy. Policy is what Granola offers; architecture is what Everpaper is.

Note quality and meeting UX#

Verdict: Granola. Honestly.

Granola's note quality is the most praised in the category; Nat Friedman said "it generates the best meeting notes of anything I've tried so far," and long-term users report it replacing their entire note-taking workflow. The human-in-the-loop trick is smart: you jot rough bullets during the call, and the AI merges them with the transcript into polished notes. That gives you more say in the output than a fully auto-generated summary, and it shows.

Everpaper's meeting flow is different in kind. While the meeting runs, a floating panel shows a live waveform, your notes with an "As we go" digest, and the speaker-labeled transcript with an Ask field. After the meeting an AI provider writes an automatic Summary, and More → Extract To-Dos into Today sends action items to Today's focus. It's a working surface more than a writing surface. If your benchmark is "the notes read like a colleague wrote them," Granola wins today.

Speaker attribution and verification#

Verdict: Everpaper.

Granola's desktop transcript labels speakers as "Me" (your mic, green bubbles) and "Them" (system audio, grey bubbles). Named speaker tags exist only for Google Meet (browser extension) and Zoom (macOS), but not Teams or anything else. And because Granola stores no audio, ever, you cannot play a moment back to check a quote. tldv called this "the no-recording catch," and it's the complaint that keeps surfacing in reviews. In a client dispute, "the AI wrote it down" without audio is a weak position.

Everpaper records mic and system audio as separate time-aligned tracks, shows live speaker labels, and keeps the audio; Save Audio is a first-class export. A meeting's Speakers tab (⌘4) can hide your voice or any speaker from that meeting's transcript view, and the hide survives re-analysis. It isn't deletion: audio and exports keep everyone. If someone must not be on tape, don't record. MOSS's honest edges: word-level timestamps inside a sentence are approximate, and a long-silent voice can re-enter under a new speaker label.

Scope: meetings vs the rest of your day#

Verdict: depends what you came for, but Everpaper covers more ground.

Granola does one thing: meetings. It does them well. Its web app is view/edit only; it cannot capture or transcribe, and it cannot import pre-recorded audio files.

Everpaper is a daybook. Day Journal takes a full-screen screenshot every 2 minutes by default (or, in Activity only mode, keeps an app-and-window timeline with no screenshots), OCRs it on-device, and, with an AI provider, writes the day up when you stop. Screenshots are kept 30 days by default; on a new install the day's text and written entry expire with them unless you choose Forever or turn off Also delete their text & recall data. Meetings aren't affected. Memory (also called Second Brain) builds a cross-day knowledge graph (People, Files, Sites, Topics, each tied to exact moments) with plain-language Beliefs and confidence markers, computed locally and readable with no AI provider. It gates itself: 5 days, 300 frames, 60 distinct things, 3 recurring threads before it says Ready. Ask your memory… (⇧⌘A) answers from matching days with a Sources list where every source is the exact frame: time, app, window title. Clicking opens the original screen. If you only want meeting notes, that scope is overhead. If you want "what was I looking at when the client said that," Granola has no answer at all. See how the day journal works and the meetings pillar.

Platforms and fit#

Verdict: Granola, decisively.

Granola runs on macOS and Windows, with iOS and Android apps for in-person meetings, and calendar sync for Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. Sign-in requires a Google or Microsoft account.

Everpaper is macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon only; Intel Macs are unsupported, and there's no iOS, Windows, Linux, Android, or web version in the supplied product docs. It's also in private beta with limited spots. If any of those constraints hit you, Granola is your answer and this section is the one that matters.

Use cases#

  • Consultant on Windows, 20 Zoom calls a week, wants notes that read well: Granola, Business tier. This is its home turf.
  • Counsel-adjacent or M&A conversations where audio in someone else's cloud is a non-starter: Everpaper. On-device transcription, audio kept locally.
  • Founder who wants meetings plus a searchable memory of the whole day: Everpaper. Granola ends at the meeting's edge.
  • Team lead who needs shared folders, Slack push, HubSpot sync, and central billing: Granola. Everpaper's supplied docs do not describe team billing or CRM connectors.
  • In-person meetings from a phone: Granola's mobile apps. Everpaper has no mobile story.

Honest pros and cons#

Granola pros: the best-reviewed note quality in the category; human-in-the-loop notes give you editorial control; Windows and mobile support; works on any platform via system audio; SOC 2 Type 2 with public post-mortems; fast shipping (MCP, API); strong customer roster (Brex, Linear, Intercom, PostHog, Replit).

Granola cons: audio streams to Deepgram/AssemblyAI, text to OpenAI/Anthropic; trains on anonymized data by default outside Enterprise; no audio stored means no playback, ever; speaker attribution is "Me"/"Them" outside Meet and Zoom; free tier's meeting history is limited (exact cap isn't published); one teardown scored it 66/100 over export and integration gaps.

Everpaper pros: on-device transcription, nothing leaves the Mac by default; live speaker labels, with a per-meeting option to hide a voice from the transcript view; audio kept as separate mic/system tracks; day journal and Memory around the meetings; Memory's answers checked against their sources, with unsupported sentences removed (the check isn't perfect, so the sources are shown); exports to txt, markdown, srt, vtt, json, plus a full Obsidian vault.

Everpaper cons: macOS 15+ and Apple Silicon only; no mobile, no web, no Windows; private beta with limited spots; AI features wait for a provider (local ones work); Memory needs 5 days and 300 frames before it's Ready; MOSS wants 16 GB machines to be happy.

Pricing#

Granola (per user per month, checked 2026-08-04, no annual discount shown on the pricing page): Basic free with limited meeting history; Business $14: unlimited history, integrations (Notion, Slack, HubSpot, Attio, Affinity, Zapier), MCP, API; Enterprise $35: SSO, admin controls, org-wide auto-deletion, model training off for the team. A solo user on Business pays $168 over 12 months; a 10-person team pays $1,680. The page previously listed Business at $10; it's $14 as of the check date.

Everpaper is in private beta with limited spots. There's no public price yet, and we won't quote one here.

FAQ#

Is Granola private?#

It's bot-free and SOC 2 Type 2 certified, and it doesn't store your audio. But it is not on-device: audio streams to Deepgram or AssemblyAI for transcription, text goes to OpenAI or Anthropic for summarization, and transcripts and notes are stored in Granola's US-hosted AWS. Granola also trains its own models on anonymized user data by default on free and Business tiers; you can opt out in Settings. If "private" means "nothing leaves my machine," Granola doesn't meet that bar; Everpaper is built around exactly that bar. More on our model in the privacy docs.

Does Granola store recordings?#

No — ever. Granola's own docs state it doesn't store meeting audio; it transcribes in real time on desktop (or from temporarily cached audio on mobile) and keeps only the transcript and your notes. That's a genuine privacy choice and it also means there's nothing to play back: you can't verify a quote against the recording. Everpaper keeps meeting audio locally on your Mac, with mic and system as separate tracks, until you delete the meeting. Its screenshot retention options (7/30/90 days or Forever) don't affect meetings.

Does Granola work on Teams?#

Yes — Granola captures anything playing through system audio, so Teams calls transcribe fine. The catch is speaker labels: named speaker tags exist only for Google Meet and Zoom, so a Teams transcript comes back as "Me" and "Them." Everpaper labels speakers live regardless of platform, and recognizes Teams links for auto-start.

Can Everpaper replace Granola if I only care about meetings?#

If you're on an Apple Silicon Mac and the meeting is your whole use case, Meetings covers recording, live transcript, Ask, an automatic Summary, action items, and export, all on your Mac (the AI parts with a provider you choose). What you give up is Granola's polished written-note style, Windows and mobile support, and the team integrations. What you gain is audio that never leaves the machine and a recording you can actually check.

Final recommendation#

This one's genuinely close, and the tiebreaker is your constraint, not the feature list. If you're on Windows, need mobile capture, or value Granola's human-in-the-loop notes, its $14 Business tier is the cloud option in this comparison.

If meeting audio in a third party's cloud, however carefully handled, is the thing you can't accept, Everpaper keeps transcription and meeting audio on the Mac; screenshots follow the retention period you set. You trade away platforms, polish, and immediacy; we're in private beta and we know it.
