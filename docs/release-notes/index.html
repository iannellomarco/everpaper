Release notes

What changed in each version of Everpaper, newest first.

What's new in Everpaper, newest first. During the private beta each build arrives as a download link and installs over the previous one; automatic updates begin with the first public release. See Updates & Support.

Note

Everpaper is the new name for Hindsight — same app, same data, same permissions. Entries below from before the rename describe Hindsight as it was branded at the time and are left as originally written.

Note

Entries marked beta are pre-release builds shared with testers ahead of a public version. Automatic updates never offer them.

2.0.0-b98 — beta (latest)LatestBeta#

Gemma 4 runs inside Everpaper on macOS 27.

  • Gemma 4 inside Everpaper Native. On macOS 27 Macs with 16 GB of memory or more, Everpaper Native now runs Gemma 4 E4B, the model that wrote the best evening entries in our tests. It runs inside Everpaper, so you don't need LM Studio, Ollama or any other server.
  • Clear sizes, careful switch. The download is about 5.9 GB, and the installed model takes about 8.5 GB (some tools show 7.9 GB). Setup checks there's room for both before it starts, and switches over only after Gemma has answered a first request. If the connection drops for a moment, the download retries and carries on where it stopped.
  • Setup picks it for you. On these Macs, guided setup now chooses Everpaper Native with Gemma. A running LM Studio or Ollama with a chat model is still used, and an AI setup you already have is kept. macOS 27 Macs with less than 16 GB keep Qwen3 4B; earlier macOS versions work as in b97.
  • Already on Everpaper Native with Qwen? A one-time card offers the switch to Gemma and shows its sizes. Qwen keeps answering while Gemma downloads, and its files (about 2.3 GB) stay until you remove them.
  • Native models by name. In Settings → AI & summaries, Everpaper Native lists the models on this Mac by name. You can set one up there, or remove one you no longer use.
  • A steadier engine. Prompts are formatted exactly as Gemma expects. An answer cut off at its length limit no longer garbles the next one, and output made only of internal marker tokens counts as a failure, so it never reaches your journal. Structured answers on long prompts use about 1 GB of memory instead of about 5.8 GB. One engine now stays loaded, closes after ten idle minutes, and restarts between requests if its memory grows too much.
  • Running notes are protected. If a rewrite of the day's running notes drops more than half of them, Everpaper keeps the earlier notes and adds the new ones after.
  • One-time setup cards are back. Recent betas carried a name suffix that silently held back Everpaper's one-time setup cards. This build doesn't, so you may now see the speaker-labels card b97 meant to show.
  • Known limits: Gemma in Everpaper Native hasn't been tried on a real 16 GB Mac yet. The first request after the engine starts can take around half a minute, and after a restart the next request pauses for a few seconds. Evening entries still condense busy days and drop some meeting details. When several people share your Mac's microphone, the live transcript shows them all as "Me" until the meeting ends; the saved transcript separates them.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon. Gemma 4 in Everpaper Native needs macOS 27 and 16 GB of memory or more.

2.0.0-b97-journal — betaBeta#

Your daily journal reads like a diary again, meetings get a summary, and Gemma 4 joins the local models.

  • Everpaper now needs macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later.
  • An evening entry in your voice. When you finish your day, Everpaper writes one warm, first-person entry from the day's notes and meetings, including each meeting's decisions, dates and who does what. A day cut short by a quit or crash is written up the next time Everpaper opens.
  • Entries can outlive their screenshots. With Also delete their text & recall data off (Settings → Daily history), finished entries are kept when old screenshots are cleaned up. The switch is on for new installs, so there a day's entry is removed with its screenshots after your retention period.
  • A summary for meetings. Each meeting of more than a few minutes gets a summary once it ends, shown on its page and reused in the evening entry. Longer meetings get longer summaries. This needs an AI option set up, and a cloud provider needs your consent.
  • Everyday meetings: Parakeet for words, Nemotron for speakers, live and in the saved transcript. If Nemotron can't run, Everpaper falls back to its previous speaker labels.
  • Clearer live speaker labels. A new caption line starts when the speaker changes; in our tests, words shown under the wrong speaker fell from about 31% to about 10%. When several people speak into your Mac's own microphone, the saved transcript separates them, and you stay "Me" when you're the only one talking.
  • Ready before your first meeting. Today warns you if transcription isn't set up, with one Set up button and the download size. If you skip it, the first meeting downloads the model with progress, and the audio is always recorded. A missing model no longer stops a meeting, discarded recordings stay discarded, a microphone that goes silent is restarted, and merging speakers keeps the right names.
  • Gemma 4 E4B for Macs with 24 GB or more (about 6.9 GB). Setup reuses an LM Studio or Ollama you already run instead of installing a second engine. Thinking is turned off, every downloaded file is checked, and existing setups are kept.
  • A simpler Speech page: Everyday or Detailed, your words, and an Advanced section listing the models on this Mac with their sizes and a Remove button.
  • Known limits: evening entries still condense busy days. When several people share your Mac's microphone, the live transcript shows everyone as "Me" until the meeting ends.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 15 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b96-nemotron — betaBeta#

Nemotron becomes the default for words and speaker labels on new setups.

  • New setups use Nemotron for both. New installations transcribe and label speakers with Nemotron. Parakeet is still in Settings → Speech, and an engine you already picked is kept.
  • Model setup checks every file. Setup verifies the files it downloads and prepares both the after-meeting and the live speaker models. Once prepared, it works offline.
  • Short live snippets keep their last word. A word at the edge of a short live chunk is no longer dropped.
  • No phantom extra speaker. A voice that already has a label no longer gets a second one from a later pass.
  • Microphone changes recover better. Everpaper copes better when you switch to an audio device that uses a different format.
  • Includes b95's click fix. The old MOSS introduction card is gone.
  • To use Nemotron for both on an existing setup, open Settings → Speech, choose Nemotron under Transcription and press Set up. In a small test Parakeet got more words right, so this beta doesn't claim Nemotron is more accurate.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b95-event — betaBeta#

Fixes for a crash and a hang linked to recording your clicks.

  • Click details are read safely. When Computer History notes what you clicked, it now reads that detail the way macOS expects. Reading it in the background could trip an error inside Everpaper's own windows, and was linked to a crash and a hang in b94.
  • Stopping the workday twice is safe. Stop requests are handled one at a time.
  • A failed save keeps your review. If saving the day fails, the review stays open so you can try again.
  • Click details are still recorded, and capture modes and saved data are unchanged. The crash isn't proven fixed yet; if clicking ever feels slow, report the app you were in and roughly when.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b94-event — betaBeta#

A fix for a crash when you start a meeting while your workday is being recorded.

  • Record now during a workday is steadier. While your workday is being recorded, pressing Record now switches Today straight to a "workday paused" status, instead of keeping the live control running through the change. This targets a crash seen on b93.
  • Mark Moment, Stop & Review and Discard (with confirmation) stay available. A workday you paused yourself still offers Resume and stays paused after the meeting.
  • If you stop the workday while the meeting continues, Today shows the day's writing and review status.
  • The original crash didn't reproduce on our test Mac, so treat this build as a test of the fix. After a crash, Everpaper keeps what was recorded and doesn't resume recording on its own.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b93-event — betaBeta#

Choose Everyday or Detailed meetings, and try live speaker labels.

  • Everyday or Detailed meetings. Settings → Speech now opens with two choices, explained by purpose and speed. The exact models are listed under "What runs on my Mac".
  • Live speaker labels (experimental, opt-in). For Everyday meetings, you can turn on live speaker labels after setting up a local Nemotron model of about 190 MB. People on the other end get labels during the call, which may change as it goes. When the model is unsure, captions keep Me / Others. The words still come from the usual live recognizer.
  • An optional after-meeting speaker check. A separate opt-in pass checks the speaker labels once the meeting ends.
  • Both are off by default, a recording never starts a model download, and saved meetings stay compatible.
  • Smoother sidebar and Settings. The selection highlight uses one animation instead of two, and Settings no longer rebuilds itself on every section change.
  • When reporting a wrong or flickering label, include the meeting's length, how many people spoke, and whether it was Everyday or Detailed.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b92-event — betaBeta#

The journal keeps what it has when a rewrite goes wrong.

  • A failed rewrite no longer shrinks the day. If the optional final rewrite fails or comes back much shorter, Everpaper keeps the day as its sources wrote it.
  • Meetings keep their own paragraphs. Updates from your screen no longer blend into meeting paragraphs; each meeting stays separate and labeled.
  • Automatic Episodes and Chapters are paused. No new ones are made for now; the ones you have stay.
  • History shows spans you can open. Recorded activities are grouped into stretches of time, each opening its sources.
  • Memory chat can read sources directly. When normal search comes up short, Ask reads a limited set of matching sources itself and skips anything no longer visible.
  • The journal isn't yet as good as it used to be, and search finds the right source less often than it should.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b91-event — betaBeta#

The journal keeps one running account of your day.

  • One account that grows through the day. The journal no longer writes separate summaries that each lose what came before. It carries a single running account and adds to it at each update.
  • Meetings join the day in order. Finished meetings enter the account at the time they happened, with more of what was actually said, before the evening entry is written.
  • Better meeting excerpts. When a transcript has to be shortened, Everpaper keeps opening questions, whole sentences and the "but…" that qualifies a point.
  • Changes ripple correctly. When a source changes or is deleted, the summaries built on it are refreshed together, and your own notes and titles are kept.
  • Smoother Memory and sidebar. Switching sections stutters less.
  • Existing journals aren't rewritten. The writing still depends on your local model and isn't yet as good as it used to be.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b90-event — betaBeta#

Settings, redesigned with plain names and native Mac controls.

  • Settings pages with plain names. Capture is now Daily history, Transcription & Speech is Speech, Intelligence is AI & summaries, Privacy & Data is Privacy, Shortcuts & System is General, and This Copy is About. Controls are standard Mac switches, menus and buttons, grouped into sections, and Privacy is simpler to move around.

  • System lettering for labels. Navigation, Today's labels and shared controls use the Mac's system font. Journals, transcripts and answers keep their reading typeface.
  • Sources restored from encrypted storage open correctly.
  • Includes the b89 journal fixes and the b88 speech defaults. No new download; permissions and consent are unchanged.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

2.0.0-b89-event — betaBeta#

The daily journal reads the whole day again.

  • Each update reads its own part of the day. In recent betas every journal update could reuse the day's first minutes, so the journal stopped moving early in the day. Each update now reads the stretch of time it covers.
  • Nothing is cut mid-field on the way to the AI. Each piece of evidence is sent whole or not at all, sized to what your model can read.
  • One long meeting no longer fills the entry. Meeting excerpts are kept to a fair share and placed in time, so the rest of the day still appears.
  • The journal says only what its sources show. A rewrite can't turn a request into a finished task, or a scheduling question into an email you received. Where a section can't be supported, Everpaper shows the source passage instead.
  • Text-only models don't get screenshots. With LM Studio, Everpaper checks whether the selected model can read images before sending any.
  • A failed read keeps the review open. If a source can't be read while the day is finished, the review stays open with an error instead of showing an old summary.
  • Existing journals aren't rewritten automatically. How good the writing is still depends on your local model.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

2.0.0-b88-event — betaBeta#

Meetings process faster with MOSS, your custom words help transcription, and word timing can be more precise.

  • The MOSS meeting model runs leaner. This beta uses a lighter build of the same model and no longer decodes a short first attempt twice. Earlier tests on two recordings showed about a fifth less processing time and about 30% less peak memory; one longer recording came out slightly worse. The original model remains the fallback, and a change takes effect after relaunch.
  • Custom words guide the standard engine. Words you add now act as gentle hints for standard transcription, so names and terms are more likely to come out right. With an empty list nothing changes.
  • Optional precise word timing. On Macs with 24 GB or more, an optional 1.8 GB model refines when each word starts and ends, after transcription. It downloads only when you press Prepare, adds some processing time, and never changes the words or who said them.
  • The end of a recording is never skipped. Audio reading continues through short reads, so the last part of a recording can't be silently left out.
  • LM Studio answers straight away with Qwen3.5. Everpaper switches off Qwen3.5's hidden "thinking" on LM Studio; in one test a 20-second wait became under a second. A reply that only thinks and never answers now shows a clear error instead of an empty answer.
  • These options are on by default in this beta and can be switched off in Settings. The wording when a speech-model download is paused is clearer.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires macOS 27.

With Everpaper Native, an answer kept running for up to about 35 seconds after its last word appeared, holding back its sources and the New conversation button. The engine now stops as soon as the answer ends; in testing, that wait dropped to about a tenth of a second.
  • Sources arrive with the answer. A Memory answer shows its full source list as soon as it finishes, without switching tabs.
  • Everything else is as in b86.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

2.0.0-b86-event — betaBeta#

A short introduction before setup, and questions about a meeting become conversations you can come back to.

  • An optional introduction. Before setup, a short interactive introduction shows what Everpaper remembers and how a detail you need later becomes an answer with its source. Drag through the examples or skip it.
  • Setup keeps your place. Quit halfway and setup reopens where you were; going back keeps your choices. It explains permissions, downloads and private processing before you act. You can run it again from Settings, and finishing opens Today or Meetings without starting a recording.
  • Ask a meeting in threads. Questions about a saved meeting now live in conversations you can return to: a list beside the chat on wide windows, a menu on narrow ones. The composer stays at the bottom, and an unsent draft survives switching conversations.
  • Answers show what they used. A meeting answer links to the meeting's transcript and your saved notes.
  • Commitments open in place. Clicking a commitment opens a small popover beside it with its title, status and source. Escape closes the source first, then the popover.
  • Meeting status takes less room. Processing status sits on the meeting's details line, with Retry visible when something fails. The Transcript, Overview, Ask and Speakers tabs are slimmer.
  • Recall connects a promise to what followed. Asking the Concordance about a commitment now cites both the meeting where it was made and the later work on it.
  • Sources stay correct when a meeting is reprocessed or corrected, or a source is deleted.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

2.0.0-b85-event — betaBeta#

Computer History now records what you do by default once you opt in, and Detailed screen history can't record less than Screen history.

  • One opt-in now covers what you do and what you type. Turn on Computer History once and it records clicks, shortcuts, short selections and encrypted typed text in ordinary apps and sites, unless you exclude them. If you already use it, nothing widens until you choose. Password fields and private browsing are never recorded.
  • Detailed screen history can't record less than Screen history. It keeps the regular full-screen snapshots, then adds a closer look at the active window after real changes, pauses in typing, or a Moment you mark. Without Accessibility permission, only those extras stop.
  • Fewer near-identical screenshots. Routine snapshots are smaller, and frames that look the same as the last one are dropped before anything is stored or read. Moments you mark keep full quality.
  • Quiet stretches stay short. A long run of switching between windows no longer gets written up as if it were real work.
  • Simpler AI choice in setup. "Choose for me" picks Everpaper Native with MOSS on macOS 27 and a managed local engine with MOSS on earlier systems. "I manage local AI" keeps every option visible. Nothing downloads until you press Prepare.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b84 — betaBeta#

Computer History arrives, with a new Adaptive mode, a History that reads as work, and a clearer first run.

  • Computer History (tester builds only). An opt-in history of what you do on your Mac: apps and windows, clicks and shortcuts, and — only if you allow it — encrypted typed text. Password fields and private browsing are never recorded, you can exclude apps and sites, and it's stored only on this Mac.
  • Adaptive mode reacts to real changes. Adaptive (today's Detailed screen history) follows the apps and windows you use, and takes an image of the window you're working in only when what you're doing changes and every privacy check passes. In this build it took no full-screen screenshots. The text in those images is read on this Mac.
  • History reads as work. A day's History now leads with Episodes: short write-ups of what you worked on, each with a title, the apps involved, how long it took and any Moments you marked. The raw list of app switches and clicks is still there underneath, as detail.
  • A clearer first run. A new welcome explains Everpaper before setup starts. The first choices separate what Everpaper remembers from the permissions setup will ask for, and the history step is wider, showing what each choice keeps and what it never records, with a summary that updates as you choose.
  • Honest about thin days. Vague “Moving between…” rows are replaced with what was actually seen or typed, when that's available. When there's too little to go on, Everpaper says so instead of inventing a task or outcome.

This tester build includes evaluation-only Computer History modes; ordinary builds don't include them.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b83 — betaBeta#

First-run setup now asks which intelligence route you want instead of deciding for you.

  • Setup is three screens instead of eleven. Choose what Everpaper should remember — your meetings, your days, or both — and setup asks only for what that choice needs.
  • Every usable route stays visible. Everpaper Native leads on macOS 27, with Managed oMLX, detected local providers, your own endpoint, and no AI all available from the same screen. The redundant Apple Intelligence row is gone; its icon now identifies Everpaper Native.
  • Existing local setups are reused. Setup can rescan providers, preserves the chosen route through the final receipt, and reuses installed oMLX and model files instead of downloading them again.
  • Local model answers stay concise. Qwen and LFM2.5 models managed by oMLX now have thinking disabled at the chat-template level, so internal reasoning does not consume the answer.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b82 — betaBeta#

Meetings use MOSS by default, and the built-in intelligence path is easier to choose.

  • MOSS is now the default meeting engine. First-run setup names the one-time download before it begins, starts it only when you press the button, and keeps working in the background while setup continues. The standard transcription pipeline remains available as a fallback until MOSS is ready.
  • Everpaper Native leads on macOS 27. The built-in engine is now the recommended intelligence choice during setup; a separate local engine remains available as an alternative. System instructions also use the model's real privileged system role.
  • Very short recordings are handled honestly. Recordings under five seconds are discarded instead of producing empty meetings, with a clear explanation in the recording pill.
  • Completed meetings no longer leave a stale progress row. The meeting list reconciles its live finalization state against the saved record after every refresh.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. Everpaper Native additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b81 — betaBeta#

The native engine gets its introduction.

  • A one-time card on macOS 27. If your Mac can run Everpaper Native (the in-app engine b80 introduced), Everpaper now tells you so once, and offers to set everything up: it downloads the model, switches your provider, and proves a first response before saying done. Decline and nothing changes; the option stays in Settings.
  • The setup survives closing the card — an accepted download keeps going in the background.
  • On macOS 26 and earlier nothing appears; the card waits until the Mac can actually use it.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. The Everpaper Native option additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b80 — betaBeta#

The first build with an engine made only of Apple frameworks — no separate AI app required.

  • New intelligence option: Everpaper Native (macOS 27). On Macs running the macOS 27 beta, the provider picker gains an "Everpaper Native" row. Choosing it runs summaries, extraction, and chat through an engine bundled with the app and built on Apple's frameworks — no LM Studio, no Ollama, no server to install or keep running. On earlier macOS versions nothing changes and the row does not appear.
  • The native engine ships with structured output built in (summaries and action items decode against a schema, not hopeful parsing) and streams its responses.
  • This beta's supported model is Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, prepared for Apple's runtime.
  • Everything else — recording, transcription, journal — is unchanged from b79 and continues to use the existing engines regardless of this setting.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. The Everpaper Native option additionally requires the macOS 27 beta.

2.0.0-b79 — betaBeta#

Same app as b78, rebuilt and signed on new hardware.

  • No functional changes. This build exists to move Everpaper's release process onto a new machine; everything listed under b78 is included and unchanged. If you are already running b78, there is nothing new to try.
  • Verified before release: notarized by Apple, signature and update-key checks passed, and the full test suite green.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b78 — betaBeta#

Pick your local AI model — or let Everpaper pick for your Mac.

  • A model choice in first-run setup. Under "Set it up for me", setup now names the model it will install and lets you change it. Every option shows its download size and how much memory it uses on a long meeting — not just the file size.
  • The recommendation fits your hardware. Everpaper reads this Mac's memory and only offers models it can actually hold alongside transcription. LFM2.5 2.6B stays the default everywhere; a faster lightweight option is always available, and Macs with 24 GB or more can pick a larger Qwen model for higher-quality summaries.
  • Bigger models answer properly now. The larger Qwen options had a habit of "thinking out loud" — pages of reasoning instead of the answer. Setup now configures them to answer directly.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b76 · b77 — betaBeta#

Local AI setup gets a better brain, fitted to your Mac. The two builds are identical; b76 expires after 15 days, b77 never does.

  • A new starter model for the one-click local AI setup. Choosing "set it up for me" now installs LFM2.5 2.6B — about half the download of the previous starter, faster on long transcripts, and tuned for exactly what this app asks of it: summarizing, extracting, and answering from your own recordings.
  • Lighter Macs get a lighter model. On a MacBook Neo the setup installs a smaller, faster model instead, so summaries finish quickly on passive cooling.
  • Reasoning models stop thinking out loud. Models that emit their scratch work before the answer no longer leak it into summaries and notes, whichever local server you use.
  • Local AI setup now needs about 6 GB free instead of 10.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b75 — betaBeta#

Settings opens instantly again.

  • Opening Settings no longer freezes the app. Since b72, opening Settings could pin the processor and beachball the whole app for ten seconds or more — on some Macs indefinitely. A bookkeeping write fired on every screen refresh while the page was open, and each write forced another refresh. The page now reads that record without touching it, and the loop is gone.

1.0.0Release#

The first release of Hindsight: a private, on-device daybook for macOS. One continuous book you turn through page by page, with three pillars and an Index (⌘K) that jumps anywhere.

Day Journal#

A running record of your day, woven from periodic screen capture read with on-device OCR (Apple Vision). Turn between days with the left and right arrows, let the red Ribbon bring you back to today, and use Ask the book on any page to pose a question about your day or your history. Screenshots and the journal database never leave your Mac.

Meetings#

Record and transcribe meetings on-device. Start one from the Record tile, the menu bar, the Index (Begin minutes), or the ⇧⌘M hotkey. Hindsight can watch your calendar and offer to begin a recording when a meeting starts, recognizing Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams links.

  • Records your microphone and your Mac's system audio as separate, time-aligned tracks (the default; System Audio Only is also available).
  • A floating pill and a live panel with Notes, a speaker-labeled Transcript, and an Ask tab.
  • Saved meetings keep their transcript, notes, and any Ask conversation, and can be renamed and re-run through prompt templates.

Second Brain#

A page that reads across every day you've captured, links the things that keep recurring, and forms plain-language beliefs about your focus. It moves between Chat, Insights, Network, and Export. The beliefs, Insights, and Network are computed locally and work with no AI provider connected; recall grounds every chat answer in your real history with citations back to the moment each claim came from.

On-device by default#

  • Capture, transcription, and storage stay local. Meetings are transcribed on your Mac with the on-device speech model (Parakeet, which runs on the Neural Engine), and your journal database never leaves it.
  • No AI traffic without a provider you choose. Until you connect an AI provider, nothing is sent anywhere — Ask the book and the Second Brain simply wait.
  • A local provider keeps everything on your machine. Connect Ollama or LM Studio and your text is processed locally.
  • Cloud is opt-in. Sending anything to a cloud provider is off until you turn it on, and the same gate applies uniformly to journal text, meeting transcripts, chat, and drafts.

Requirements#

  • macOS 14.2 or later.
  • Apple Silicon (M-series) — the on-device speech model runs on the Neural Engine.
  • A few gigabytes of free disk space for the speech model, which downloads once during first-time setup.
