Release notes

What changed in each version of Everpaper, newest first.

What's new in Everpaper, newest first. Everpaper keeps itself up to date, and you can check manually from the Colophon under The Apparatus → Check for updates.

Everpaper is the new name for Hindsight — same app, same data, same permissions. Entries below from before the rename describe Hindsight as it was branded at the time and are left as originally written.

Entries marked beta are pre-release builds shared with testers ahead of a public version. Automatic updates never offer them.

2.0.0-b76 · b77 — beta

Local AI setup gets a better brain, fitted to your Mac. The two builds are identical; b76 expires after 15 days, b77 never does.

  • A new starter model for the one-click local AI setup. Choosing "set it up for me" now installs LFM2.5 2.6B — about half the download of the previous starter, faster on long transcripts, and tuned for exactly what this app asks of it: summarizing, extracting, and answering from your own recordings.
  • Lighter Macs get a lighter model. On a MacBook Neo the setup installs a smaller, faster model instead, so summaries finish quickly on passive cooling.
  • Reasoning models stop thinking out loud. Models that emit their scratch work before the answer no longer leak it into summaries and notes, whichever local server you use.
  • Local AI setup now needs about 6 GB free instead of 10.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b75 — beta

Settings opens instantly again.

  • Opening Settings no longer freezes the app. Since b72, opening Settings could pin the processor and beachball the whole app for ten seconds or more — on some Macs indefinitely. A bookkeeping write fired on every screen refresh while the page was open, and each write forced another refresh. The page now reads that record without touching it, and the loop is gone.
  • Nothing else changed in this build; it exists to get the fix out fast. The b74 improvements below are all included.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b74 — beta

The wait after a meeting stops being a mystery: what step, how long, and one click to the details.

  • The pill is clickable now — in every state. While a transcription runs, clicking the floating pill takes you straight to the Meetings page, where the working row shows the step, the percentage, and the time estimate. When it says "Saved", clicking opens the finished meeting. It used to swallow the click silently; right-click and VoiceOver now offer the same routes.
  • Every step has a name. Preparing the audio, transcribing, separating speakers, saving — the pill, the meetings row, and the Today page now say which one is running. Before this build, only the transcription itself reported anything, so the bar could sit at 100% for minutes while real work happened in silence.
  • "About 6 min left." The transcription now estimates its remaining time from its own measured pace — shown in the meetings list, on the Today page, and during a re-analysis. It appears only once there is enough real progress to be honest, and disappears rather than guess.
  • The percentage no longer vanishes mid-way. On a two-track recording the number used to disappear for minutes at the halfway mark; it now carries through.
  • A checkmark means done now. The re-analysis view used to show its checkmark when the transcription finished, while speaker separation and saving were still running.
  • Smaller honesty fixes throughout. The menu bar says "Transcribing…" instead of a disabled "Stop Recording"; an in-flight transcription no longer disappears from the list while you type in search; a long error message fits inside the pill instead of being cut off; and a rare stall in speaker separation can no longer hold a finished transcript hostage.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b73 — beta

A sweep through everything the app wasn't saying out loud.

  • Deleting a meeting can no longer half-delete it. The audio was removed before the database entry, so a delete that failed partway left a meeting you could still see but never play. The order is reversed, and a failure now says so instead of pretending nothing happened.
  • A recording that dies mid-meeting says so. If the microphone drops or the disk fills, the saved meeting now says it ended early — it used to report "Saved" exactly like a normal stop, leaving you to discover the missing forty minutes on your own.
  • The expired-beta message actually appears everywhere. In b72, an expired build refused to start the day journal silently — the Start button just did nothing. It now explains itself there too, and Settings keeps saying a build has expired instead of going quiet at that exact moment. (This build does not expire.)
  • Re-analysis shows its percentage on every engine. The progress bar was still wired to the old assumption that only one engine could report.
  • The menu bar stops claiming "Capturing" during a meeting. It now says "Paused for meeting", matching what the app actually does.
  • Quieter, more careful internals. File names no longer land in the system log where a diagnostics bundle could carry them; the command-line tools run stored transcripts through the same credential-stripping pass as the app; deleting a day keeps its screenshots restorable for the full undo window; and several heavy list and audio operations got lighter.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b72 — beta

Progress you can see on every engine, and a beta that knows when to stop.

  • The other two transcription engines report their progress now. Until this build only the advised meeting model showed a percentage; the standard pipeline — the one most people are on — spun with nothing to say for the whole wait. Both now count up, in the pill and in the meetings list. They report a plain percentage rather than naming the part of the recording being worked on, so it is coarser than the advised model, but it moves.
  • This beta stops recording after fifteen days. A beta never updates itself, so an old copy would otherwise drift on forever, unsupported. When the date passes, Everpaper declines to start a new recording and points you at the current build.
  • Nothing is ever locked away. An expired beta is read-only, not closed: your meetings, your days, search, and export all keep working exactly as before. The recordings on your Mac are yours, and no expiry touches them.
  • You get warned first. Three days out, Settings opens with a line saying the build is about to stop recording, and a button that takes you to the current one.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b71 — beta

Recordings that finish themselves, and a wait you can watch.

  • A recording interrupted mid-transcript now finishes on its own. If Everpaper is quit or killed while it is still writing a transcript, the audio was always safe — but finishing it meant knowing to open Meetings and press Recover. Everpaper now rebuilds it the next time you open the app, and tells you it did. When it decides not to, it says so rather than leaving you to find out.
  • It gives each recording one automatic try. If rebuilding a particular recording is what stopped the app, Everpaper will not keep walking into that at every launch — that one waits for you to choose, and everything else still rebuilds on its own.
  • It stands aside for the work in front of you. Automatic rebuilding never begins during a meeting, stops if you start one, and holds off while your Mac is in Low Power Mode.
  • The pill counts up. After you stop a meeting, the floating pill now reads "Transcribing… 47%" instead of spinning with nothing to say. The percentage comes from the advised meeting model; the standard pipeline still shows a plain spinner.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

2.0.0-b70 — beta

The meeting you just stopped no longer vanishes while it is written.

  • A meeting being transcribed appears in the list while it works. Until it finished, a just-stopped meeting existed nowhere — not in Meetings, not in the Library — so a long transcription looked like nothing was happening at all. It now appears immediately, carrying its own progress, and becomes the finished meeting when it is done.
  • Described for what it is. That entry reads "Transcribing", not "Recovering" — recovery wording is kept for a recording genuinely orphaned by a crash, so it still means something when you see it.
  • The day journal stops saying it is recording when it is not. Screen capture pauses while a meeting records; the journal now says so, instead of showing "Recording" above a frame count that cannot move. Marking a moment still works throughout.
  • Capture that quietly fails is now visible. A tick that captures frames but cannot store them, or a second display that stops producing frames, is reported rather than passing for healthy.
  • A long transcription reports its progress to the system log. If one ever does get stuck, there is now a record that tells a working decode apart from a stalled one.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.16.0

One model for meetings: words and voices together.

  • Meet MOSS, the new advised meeting model. Until now, Hindsight transcribed meetings in two passes — one model wrote the words, a second worked out who spoke, and the two were stitched together afterwards. MOSS (MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize, Apache 2.0) does both at once: a single on-device model that transcribes and tells voices apart as it listens, so speaker changes land where sentences change hands. It is advised for meetings and entirely opt-in — about a 2 GB one-time download, and audio never leaves your Mac.
  • New users meet it during setup. First-run setup gains a "Tell Speakers Apart" step: accept to download MOSS in the background while you keep walking through setup, or decline and keep the standard pipeline — nothing blocks, and you can change your mind later in Settings.
  • Existing users get one quiet announcement. On first launch after updating, a single "New in this impression" notice explains the model and offers to set it up in place, with live download progress. Dismiss it and it never returns — a Press Check line and the new Meeting Model entry under Settings → Transcription & Speech remain the permanent path.
  • Switching back is one click. "Use instead" returns meetings to the standard Parakeet pipeline at any time; the downloaded model is never deleted, and typing, dictation, and file transcription keep using Parakeet or Whisper exactly as before.
  • Long meetings stay light. MOSS processes audio in ten-minute windows and stitches speakers across them, so memory stays flat (about 5 GB during finalization) no matter how long the meeting runs. The live meeting pane keeps its instant preview; the final pass after you stop is the authoritative transcript.
  • Honest edges. Word-level timestamps inside a sentence are approximate with MOSS (segment boundaries are exact), and a voice that stays silent for a very long stretch may re-enter under a new speaker label. Raw and Clean Lexicon modes apply to MOSS transcripts exactly as they do everywhere else.
  • For the terminal. hindsight-cli transcribe --engine moss --diarize runs the same joint model on any audio file.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. The meeting model is happiest on 16 GB machines.

1.15.1

Raw now means raw for meetings too.

  • Meeting transcripts honor the Lexicon mode. Raw leaves recognized meeting wording untouched; Clean applies your Custom Words corrections during meeting finalization, crash recovery, and Re-analyze.
  • Re-analyze stays consistent. When you rerun transcription and speaker separation on a completed meeting, Hindsight uses the current Raw/Clean setting instead of silently applying corrections every time.
  • The Lexicon is clearer. The Processing mode copy now says it applies to both file transcriptions and meeting transcripts, and Custom Words are described as Clean-mode corrections.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.15.0

One click, one brain: setup can now install your local AI for you.

  • Download & Set Up — and that's it. If no local AI server is running, first-time setup now offers to do everything: it installs oMLX (a free, notarized inference server built for Apple Silicon), downloads the Qwen3.5 4B model (~4 GB) from Hugging Face with live progress, starts the server, and connects Hindsight the moment the model is ready. No account, no API key, no terminal — and nothing ever leaves your Mac.
  • Interruption-proof. A dropped connection doesn't restart the 4 GB download — setup resumes from the files that already finished. If oMLX's own first-run screen holds things up, Hindsight starts the server directly so setup still completes on its own.
  • Your server, your choice. Prefer LM Studio? One click opens its download page and Hindsight connects automatically as soon as its server appears. Already running Ollama, LM Studio, or oMLX? Setup finds it and connects with one click, exactly as before. And on Macs with Apple Intelligence available, there's a zero-download Connect that uses the on-device Apple model.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

A design release. Every page got a pass from an expert review of the whole interface — smarter suggestions, honest insights, readable type, and dozens of paper cuts gone.

Tell Hindsight your email addresses (Settings → Second Brain) and it stops mistaking your own name for a person to track — no more "assess your own relevance" suggestions. Speculative observations can't become Apple Reminders anymore, websites and people no longer masquerade as tasks, and the same insight stops appearing twice in different words.

Chat now owns its tab with one scroll instead of a scroll-inside-a-scroll, and duplicated model answers collapse automatically.

Rapid-fire crosstalk renders compactly, phantom "speakers" with five words step out of the speaker list, transcript previews read like sentences, prompts show as runnable actions and replace their old results instead of stacking duplicates.
  • Days that read like a day. Timeline entries describe what was actually on screen (never keyword soup), quick message-checking hops fold into one "Checking messages" row, and a new MARGINALIA column surfaces the day's open questions and loose ends — only when there are any, and never invented.
  • Easier on the eyes. Long AI answers now read in the book's serif with proper line spacing instead of typewriter-style text, numbered lists align properly everywhere, and the Archive gained date groups, clickable favorite stars, a clearer filter bar, and word-by-word search.

1.8.2

A quick follow-up to the file-indexing rework: live status you can actually watch, and proper patience with local AI models that are still loading.

  • Indexing shows its work again. The progress bar appears the moment indexing starts — no more long silent stretch on a fresh index — and a live status line tells you exactly what's happening: "Reading files…", "Splitting files by meaning…", "Embedding chunks (batch 2/7)…". Pause and Cancel are always visible while a run is active, and pausing now takes effect mid-run instead of waiting for the current batch of files to finish.
  • Cold models are waited for, not skipped. If your local AI server is still loading a model when indexing starts, Hindsight now waits for it — retrying for a couple of minutes with a "Waiting for the embedding model to load…" status — instead of silently falling back to the weaker built-in embedder for the whole run. Meaning-aware chunking gets the same fix: a model that takes more than a minute to load no longer loses semantic splitting for every file.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.8.1

A focused reliability release for reminders — the Today rail's suggested to-dos and calendar meeting alerts.

  • "Add to Reminders" works now. Exporting your to-dos to Apple Reminders crashed the app the moment macOS asked for permission — a packaging bug, fixed and now guarded by a test. If access is denied, Hindsight explains why and offers an Open System Settings shortcut instead of failing silently.

Reminders now show as banners while Hindsight is the frontmost app (they were silently suppressed before), a Mac that slept through the reminder window still alerts you on wake while the meeting is joinable — with honest timing ("starts in 2 minutes" / "has started") — and declining notification permission no longer switches the whole calendar feature off.
  • Fresher suggestions. Meeting action items older than a week stop resurfacing on Today as if they were new.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.8.0

File indexing got a ground-up rework — dramatically faster and easier on your Mac — and your indexed files now back up the Second Brain's answers directly.

  • File indexing is fast now. Folders index in parallel waves instead of one file at a time, re-indexing an unchanged folder finishes in seconds with zero wasted work, and a single huge file can no longer stall the run. On machines running a local AI, indexing also stops swapping models back and forth for every file — the biggest source of slowdown.
  • Answers cite your files. Ask the Second Brain something that lives in an indexed folder and the answer now draws on those files, with [file:n] citations pointing at the exact file and lines it used.
  • Snappier everywhere. Sending a question paints instantly instead of waiting on retrieval, the Second Brain tab opens without a hitch, and recall, day chats, and the Days list no longer block the interface while they read the journal.
  • Retention that means it. A new toggle — "Also delete their text & recall data" — makes the retention setting remove expired captures' text, search index, and recall entities too, so old data truly expires and the database stays bounded. It's on for new installs; existing journals keep their history unless you opt in. Cleanup now also runs daily, not just when a session starts.
  • Meetings, tightened. Stereo playback now lines up with the transcript timeline, and the live preview cuts at natural speech pauses instead of mid-word. Plus a batch of fixes: the AI vision-descriptions toggle actually works now, importing from the legacy app no longer freezes the window, and chat vs. embeddings API keys no longer overwrite each other.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.7.0

Export your meeting transcripts, get real speakers out of single-microphone recordings, and watch — and pause — file indexing while it runs.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.6.0

A more AI-driven Second Brain — pick your own embedding model, smarter suggested to-dos, and meaning-aware file indexing — plus a wave of reliability fixes across reminders, your days, and the Second Brain.

  • Choose your embedding model — and it's used everywhere. The embedding model you pick for Folder Context RAG now drives both indexing and search, so your files and your queries are always embedded with the same model (a mismatch used to silently break semantic search). Point it at a local model in Ollama, LM Studio, or oMLX and nothing leaves your Mac; a cloud embedding model only ever receives your file text if you explicitly choose one and grant consent.
  • Smarter suggested to-dos. Today's task suggestions are rephrased into natural, imperative tasks; meeting action items are extracted by the model from the meeting text; and tasks you pull from a specific meeting now stay on the rail when the page refreshes. Each step falls back to the built-in, on-device behavior when no model is connected.
  • Meaning-aware file chunking. With a local model configured, files are split into sections by meaning instead of by paragraph or page, so retrieval lands on coherent passages — and it falls back cleanly to structural chunking otherwise. PDFs and spreadsheets keep their page-accurate citations.
  • File search actually uses your vectors. Semantic file search now runs whenever you've typed a query (multi-word queries used to silently drop to keyword-only), re-indexes only the files that changed instead of re-embedding the whole folder every time, can be cancelled mid-run, and tells you when a folder has nothing to index.
  • Reminders that behave. "Add to Reminders" confirms when it worked, won't pile up duplicates, and writes the whole list in one pass — and an imminent meeting's reminder is no longer dropped when notification permission was still warming up.
  • Faster, more honest day pages. Opening a day no longer hitches the interface, screenshots use far less memory, the timeline shows the whole day (not just the first stretch of it), per-app time and on-disk storage are reported accurately, and deleting a day takes a single confirmation.
  • A steadier Second Brain. Beliefs no longer fade away prematurely, so your self-model and persona stay populated; chat citations resolve to the right frames after you reopen a conversation; and the self-model keeps updating even in Low Power Mode.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.5.2

Debugging tools for the embeddings provider — so you can tell when your model isn't loaded and fix it without reading server logs.

  • Test Connection for embeddings. The embeddings provider card now has a "Test Connection" button (just like the chat AI). It sends a tiny embed request to your configured endpoint and tells you whether it worked — including the served model and vector dimensions — or shows the exact error. No more guessing whether your model is loaded or the name is right.
  • Diagnostic logging. The embeddings path now logs to Console.app (subsystem com.iannellomarco.hindsight.core, category Embeddings + FileIndexer). You can see exactly what model was requested, whether it succeeded, and when the app fell back to the on-device model — filter Console by the subsystem and send the logs when something's off.

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon.

1.5.1

A dedicated embeddings model picker for Folder Context RAG — and three fixes the adversarial code review caught before they reached you.

  • Pick your embeddings model. Folder Context RAG now has its own provider config in Settings → Intelligence → File Index, parallel to your chat AI. Point it at Harrier OSS v1 0.6B (or any embedding model) in your Ollama, LM Studio, or oMLX instance — independent of your chat model. A refresh-from-provider list helps you pick. With none set, files use the on-device model.
  • Saves take effect immediately. Configuring the embeddings model used to require an app relaunch (a stale-capture bug froze the old config). Now the index and retrieval read the live setting on every call — change the model, hit re-index, and it's used right away.
  • File content never leaves your Mac. The fallback path that used your chat AI for embeddings is now gated to local providers only — a cloud chat provider will never receive your file text for embedding.
  • Retrieval uses the right model. A matching fix ensures the query vectors and the indexed vectors come from the same model, so semantic search actually works (it was silently mismatched before).

Requires macOS 14.2 or later, Apple Silicon. We recommend Harrier OSS v1 0.6B (the MLX 8-bit build) in your Ollama or LM Studio instance for file embeddings.

1.5.0

Your Second Brain now leads with genuine AI insight, reads your own files, and works reliably on local models — all on-device, all private.

Now the AI Reflection — grounded observations written from your real captured moments — leads the page, and the templated belief cards are rephrased into varied, specific observations by the model. Point Hindsight at a workspace folder — a repo, a docs folder, a papers folder — and it indexes everything inside on your Mac: code, markdown, PDFs, images (via OCR), spreadsheets, rich text, email. Hindsight now asks your local model (Ollama, LM Studio, oMLX) for structured JSON output instead of prompting and hoping — the change that makes a 3–8B local model dependable as your main Second Brain, not a fallback. Cloud stays optional and opt-in.
  • The Reflection checks itself, and sees the right frames. Every reflection is now verified against your actual captured data before it sticks — a fabricated name paired with a real citation gets stripped. Your Second Brain now genuinely thinks about your work — and the whole app got sharper, more honest, and more consistent.

1.0.0

The first release of Hindsight: a private, on-device daybook for macOS. One continuous book you turn through page by page, with three pillars and an Index (⌘K) that jumps anywhere.

Day Journal

A running record of your day, woven from periodic screen capture read with on-device OCR (Apple Vision). Turn between days with the left and right arrows, let the red Ribbon bring you back to today, and use Ask the book on any page to pose a question about your day or your history. Screenshots and the journal database never leave your Mac.

Meetings

Record and transcribe meetings on-device. Start one from the Record tile, the menu bar, the Index (Begin minutes), or the ⇧⌘M hotkey. Hindsight can watch your calendar and offer to begin a recording when a meeting starts, recognizing Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams links.

  • Records your microphone and your Mac's system audio as separate, time-aligned tracks (the default; System Audio Only is also available).
  • A floating pill and a live panel with Notes, a speaker-labeled Transcript, and an Ask tab.
  • Saved meetings keep their transcript, notes, and any Ask conversation, and can be renamed and re-run through prompt templates.

Second Brain

A page that reads across every day you've captured, links the things that keep recurring, and forms plain-language beliefs about your focus. It moves between Chat, Insights, Network, and Export. The beliefs, Insights, and Network are computed locally and work with no AI provider connected; recall grounds every chat answer in your real history with citations back to the moment each claim came from.

On-device by default

  • Capture, transcription, and storage stay local. Meetings are transcribed on your Mac with the on-device speech model (Parakeet, which runs on the Neural Engine), and your journal database never leaves it.
  • No AI traffic without a provider you choose. Until you connect an AI provider, nothing is sent anywhere — Ask the book and the Second Brain simply wait.
  • A local provider keeps everything on your machine. Connect Ollama or LM Studio and your text is processed locally.
  • Cloud is opt-in. Sending anything to a cloud provider is off until you turn it on, and the same gate applies uniformly to journal text, meeting transcripts, chat, and drafts.

Requirements

  • macOS 14.2 or later.
  • Apple Silicon (M-series) — the on-device speech model runs on the Neural Engine.
  • A few gigabytes of free disk space for the speech model, which downloads once during first-time setup.
