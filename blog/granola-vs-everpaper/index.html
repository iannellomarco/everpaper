Short answer

Everpaper is the better Granola alternative if you want audio and transcripts kept on your Mac, with the audio saved so you can replay any moment. Granola is better if you need Windows, a phone app or team sharing, or you prefer writing your own notes during the call.

Disclosure. Everpaper is our product, and it is in this article. We say where it falls short. We checked every competitor fact in November 2026 against the pages listed under Sources.

Facts as of November 2026. Prices and features change. Each source is linked at the end.

People searching for something better than Granola usually want one of two things: the same bot-free notes without the cloud, or more than meetings. This page is about both, and about the cases where Granola is still the right answer.

Granola

Granola captures your microphone and system audio on the desktop, with no bot in the call, and turns your notes and the transcript into a summary. Its security page says “For transcription and AI summarization, Granola uses secure cloud processing”, names transcription providers like Deepgram and Assembly and AI providers like OpenAI and Anthropic, and says it does not store the audio. Notes are stored in a US-hosted AWS environment. Third parties may not train on your data. Granola trains on anonymized data unless you opt out in Settings, and on Enterprise that is off by default.

Prices on its pricing page: Basic $0 with 30 days of history, Business $14 per user a month, Enterprise $35, all with desktop, iOS, Android and Apple Watch apps. The desktop app runs on macOS (14 or later) and Windows, according to its setup docs. We did not find a statement that transcription works offline.

Everpaper

Everpaper is our product. Press ⇧⌘M and it records your microphone and the call’s system audio as separate tracks, then transcribes and labels speakers on your Mac. No bot joins. Recordings are never uploaded to a server, and the audio stays on your Mac so you can replay any moment from the transcript. Each finished meeting has the transcript and, with an AI provider, a summary and an Ask tab about that meeting.

What sets it apart from the other meeting apps is the rest of the day. If you start the day journal, it also keeps screenshots read on the Mac, and Ask can answer across your meetings and your screen history and show the screenshot or the moment in the recording. Granola’s chat also works across meetings, but it has no screen history. By default Everpaper does not start by itself: you press record for a meeting (or turn on calendar auto-start) and start the journal for a day.

The summary and the live “as we go” digest need an AI provider. A provider on your Mac keeps that text local. A cloud provider gets meeting text only after you switch on a setting that is off by default. If no provider is set up, you get the recording and transcript and no meeting text leaves your Mac.

Limits: private beta with no public price, Apple silicon Macs on macOS 15 or later, no Windows, no public phone app, no team workspace. It is not a place to write notes. Because nothing joins the call, you need to tell people you are recording.

The on-device apps that compete for this search

These are the apps that tend to come up for “Granola alternative, on-device”. We read each project’s own page; the Oaken Notes facts come from a page Oaken wrote about Granola, so treat its claims about Granola as one side.

Meetily is open source under the MIT License, with native apps for Windows and macOS on Apple silicon. It transcribes on the device with Whisper or Parakeet, and summaries can use Ollama locally or a cloud provider, in which case only transcript text is sent. The Community Edition is free; Pro is $10 per user a month, billed yearly, and adds MCP, CLI and webhook integrations. Its page describes audio capture only, not screen history.

anarlog, formerly Hyprnote (the team now builds Char), is an open-source, local-first notetaker with desktop apps for macOS, Windows and Linux and iOS and Android in beta. Speech and language models can run locally and cloud is opt-in. Some parts are commercially licensed, and its page does not list prices. Older articles use the Hyprnote name.

Oaken Notes is a Mac app that says it processes audio on the device with Apple Intelligence and WhisperKit, needs macOS 26 on Apple silicon, and has a free tier and a $9.99 monthly Pro plan. These details are Oaken’s own.

Oatmeal is a free Mac app for Apple silicon on macOS 15 or later. Its own page says speech and summaries run on the device and that audio and transcripts never leave the Mac. Transcripted is a free, MIT-licensed Mac app for Apple silicon that needs macOS 26 or later. Its page says transcription runs on your Mac and that connected AI tools may process notes you share online. Both pages are written by the vendors.

If all you want is on-device meeting notes, any of them can be enough, and Meetily and anarlog are free and open to inspect. Everpaper is for people who also want the screen history and one Ask across it, and accept a private beta to get it.

Pick Granola if

  • You use Windows, iPhone, Android or an Apple Watch as well as a Mac.
  • Cloud processing is fine for your meetings, or you prefer writing your own notes during the call.
  • You want to start today rather than apply for a beta.
  • You want integrations or team sharing, or its iOS, Android and Apple Watch apps.

Pick Everpaper if

  • Audio and transcripts must stay on your Mac, with the audio kept for replay.
  • You want meetings and the rest of your workday searchable in one place, with sources.
  • You use an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 15 or later and can wait for a private beta spot.

Frequently asked questions

Is there something better than Granola?

It depends on what you need. For audio and transcripts that never leave your Mac, the on-device apps are better: Everpaper, Meetily, anarlog (when set up with local models) and Oaken Notes. For Windows, a phone app, team sharing and an app you can use today, Granola is the better pick.

Is Granola private?

Granola says it does not store meeting audio and does not let providers such as OpenAI or Anthropic train on your data. Its security page also says transcription and summaries use cloud processing, with providers like Deepgram, Assembly, OpenAI and Anthropic, and notes are stored in a US-hosted AWS environment. Granola itself trains on anonymized data unless you opt out, except on Enterprise where it is off by default.

Does Granola work offline?

Granola says notes and meeting history are stored locally for offline viewing. Its pages say transcription and summaries use cloud processing, and we did not find a statement that they work offline.

Is there a free Granola alternative?

Granola's own Basic plan is $0 with 30 days of history. Meetily's Community Edition is free and MIT-licensed, and anarlog's community app is MIT-licensed. Oaken Notes says it has a free tier. Everpaper is in private beta and has no public price.

Is there a Granola alternative that records without a bot?

Granola itself has no bot. So do Everpaper, Meetily, anarlog, Oaken Notes, Oatmeal and Transcripted. All of them capture audio from your computer, so you tell people you are recording.

Is there a Granola alternative for Windows?

Everpaper is Mac only. Meetily has Windows and macOS apps, and anarlog lists macOS, Windows and Linux. Granola's desktop app also runs on Windows.

What does Everpaper do that Granola does not?

It transcribes on the Mac, keeps recordings from being uploaded to a server, keeps the audio on the Mac so you can replay any moment, and also keeps your workday if you start the day journal, so Ask can answer across meetings and your screen history. Granola's chat also works across meetings, but it has no screen history. Granola is built around the notes you type in a meeting, which Everpaper does not replace as an editor.

Does Everpaper make summaries without the cloud?

Summaries need an AI provider. A provider that runs on your Mac keeps the text local. A cloud provider receives meeting text only after you turn on a switch that is off by default. Without any provider you still get the recording and transcript, and no meeting text leaves your Mac.
