Short answer

Day One is the polished journal if you want to write entries, with end-to-end encryption on every plan and AI on its top tier. Obsidian, Bear and Capacities are cheaper places to write. Everpaper is different: on a Mac it writes a narrative of your workday from your screen history, so you review instead of writing.

Disclosure. Everpaper is our product, and it is in this article. We say where it falls short, and every competitor fact below carries the date we checked it.

“AI journaling” covers two products. One helps you write: prompts, summaries of entries you wrote, a chat that reflects your words back. The other writes the day from what your computer saw, and you read it at night. This list has both.

Day One

Day One is the established journaling app: a free Basic plan on one device, Silver at $49.99 a year for unlimited devices, Gold at $74.99 a year. It lists apps for iPhone, Android, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch plus web access, and end-to-end encryption on every plan. AI sits on Gold, with usage limits.

Stay with Day One if you want to write, on many devices, with encryption included.

Obsidian, Bear and Capacities

Obsidian is free without limits and keeps notes as local files, and a daily note is a common pattern. It has no capture of its own. See Obsidian alternatives.

Bear is a Markdown notes app for Mac, iPhone and iPad. Local access is free, and iCloud sync is part of Pro at $2.99 a month.

Capacities has a daily note as its entry point, with a free Basic plan.

Everpaper

Everpaper is our product. It assumes you will not write the journal. Start your workday journal and it takes a screenshot of each display every 2 minutes by default and reads it on the Mac. With an AI provider set up, it keeps running notes through the day and drafts the day’s entry, and an end-of-day review lets you correct it. After writing, it checks the dates and names in the entry against what you did that day.

It records the outer day, not feelings, so it is not a replacement for a diary. It needs macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon, has no mobile app, and is in private beta with no public price. Screenshots are deleted after 30 days by default.

Pair it with whatever you write in. It can export your days as an Obsidian vault.

How to choose

  1. You like writing and want it on every device: Day One.
  2. You want free and local: Obsidian, or Bear for a lighter Apple-only notebook.
  3. You want a structured daily note: Capacities.
  4. You never write the journal and want a record anyway: Everpaper.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Day One cost?

Day One lists Basic at $0 a year for one device, Silver at $49.99 a year and Gold at $74.99 a year, with annual prices only. Silver and Gold have unlimited devices.

Is Day One encrypted?

Day One says end-to-end encryption is included in all three plans, including the free Basic plan.

Which Day One plan has AI?

Gold adds Daily Chat, Go Deeper Prompts, Entry Summaries, Title Suggestions and Image Generation, with daily usage limits. Silver includes Day One Labs access. Basic lists no AI features.

What is an AI journal that writes itself?

Everpaper writes a narrative of your workday on a Mac from what your screen history captured, once you set up an AI provider, and you review it at the end of the day. An on-device provider keeps the text on the Mac.

Is there a free Day One alternative?

Obsidian is free without limits, with daily notes as a core feature. Bear has a free tier with local access. Capacities Basic is free with a daily note.
